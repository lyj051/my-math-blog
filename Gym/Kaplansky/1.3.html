Lemma 1

Let \(R\subset T\) be domains. In saying that \(T\) is algebraic over \(R\), we mean that every element of \(T\) is algebraic over \(R\); this is equivalent to saying that the quotient field of \(T\) is algebraic over the quotient field of \(R\).

Lemma 2

  • Let \(R\) be a domain. Suppose that \(a\) is in a larger domain and is algebraic over \(R\). Then \(R[a]\) is algebraic

  • Let \(R\subset T \subset S\) be domains. If \(S\) is algebraic over \(T\) and \(T\) is algebraic over \(R\), then \(S\) is algebraic over \(R\).

Exercise 1

Let \(R\subset T\) be domains with \(T\) finitely generated as a ring over \(R\), and not algebraic over \(R\). Prove that \(T\) is not a G-domain.

Proof. Let \(T=R[t_1,\cdots , t_r,\cdots,t_n]\) where \(t_1,\cdots,t_r\) are transcendental elements over \(R\) and \(t_{r+1},\dots,t_n\) are algebraic elements over \(R\), which are algebraic over \(R[t_1,\cdots,t_r]\) all the more so. Write \[ R\subset R[t_1]\subset R[t_1,t_2] \subset \dots \subset R[t_1,\cdots,t_r] \subset T \] Assume that \(T\) is a G-domain and then so is \(R[t_1,\cdots,t_r]\). W.L.O.G. assume that \(t_r\) is transcendental over \(R[t_1,\cdots,t_{r-1}]\). Hence we get a contradiction.

Exercise 2

Let \(R\) be a domain with quotient field \(K\), and \(u\) a non-zero element of \(R\). Let \(J\) be the kernel of the homomorphism \(\mu\) from \(R[x]\) into \(K\) given by \(x\mapsto u^{-1}\). Prove: \(J=(ux-1)\).

Proof. Since \(\mu(ux-1)=0\), \((ux-1) \subseteq \ker \mu =J\).

The reverse inclusion might not be as trivial as it seems. We use induction on the degree \(n\) of \(f(x)=a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x +a_0\in J\). When \(n=1\), \(f=ax+b\); it follows from \(a+bu=0\) that \(f=b-bux=-b(ux-1)\in (ux-1)\). Assume it holds for \(n=r\). Write \[ f+a_0(ux-1)=a_{r+1}x^{r+1}+\cdots+a_0+a_0(ux-1)=xg, \qquad \deg g=r \] Since \(x\notin (ux-1)\) and \(f+a_0(ux-1)\in J\) is prime, \(g\in J\). However, by hypothesis, \(g\in (ux-1)\), hence \(f\).

Exercise 3

Let \(R\) be an integral domain having only a finite number of prime ideals. Prove that \(R\) is a G-domain.

Proof. Let \(\mathfrak{p}_1,\dots,\mathfrak{p}_r\) be non-zero prime ideals in \(R\). Choose \(0\neq a_i\in\mathfrak{p}_i\) then \(0\neq a_1\cdots a_r\in \bigcap_{i=1}^r\mathfrak{p}_i\) (since \(R\) is domain). Theorem 2 applies and the proof is complete.

Lemma 3

Let \(\mathfrak{a}_i,\, i=1,\dots,n\) be ideals in a commutative ring \(R\). Suppose that \(\mathfrak{p}\) is a prime ideal in \(R\) containing \(\bigcap_i \mathfrak{a}_i\). Then for some \(1\leq k\leq n\), we have \(\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}_k\).

Proof. Assume that \(\mathfrak{a}_k\nsubseteq \mathfrak{p}\) for each \(k\). Therefore there exists \(x_k\in \mathfrak{a}_k\) such that \(x_k\notin \mathfrak{p}\). Then \(x_1\cdots x_r\in \bigcap_i \mathfrak{a}_i\subseteq \mathfrak{p}\), a contradiction.

Corollary 4

If \(\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{a}_i\) is a prime ideal \(\mathfrak{p}\), then \(\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{a}_k\) for some \(1\leq k\leq r\).

Exercise 4

Let \(R\) be a Hilbert ring having only a finite number of maxiaml ideals. Prove that these are the only prime ideals in \(R\).

Proof. By Corollary 11, a prime ideal is the intersection of maximal ideals containing it. Since \(R\) has a finite number of maximal ideals, by Corollary above, it follows that a prime ideal is equal to some maximal ideal. Therefore, these maximal ideals are the only prime ideals in \(R\).

Exercise 5

Let \(R\) be an integral domain with quotient field \(K\). Assume that \(K\) is countably generated as a ring over \(R\). Prove that \(R\) is a G-domain if and only if \(K\) cannot be expressed as the union of a properly ascending sequence of subrings containing \(R\).

Proof. If \(R\) is a G-domain, then \(K=R[u]\) for some \(u\in K\). If \(K\) admits a form \[ K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{+\infty}R_i, \quad R_i\subsetneq R_{i+1}, R_i\subsetneq K, \, \forall i \] then \(u\in R_i\) for some \(i\). It follows that \(R[u]\subseteq R_i\subsetneq K\), a contradiction.

Conversely, assume that \(K\) cannot be expressed as the union of a properly ascending sequence of subrings containing \(R\). Write \(K=R[(a_j)_{j\in \mathbb{N}}]\). Clearly we have \[ K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{+\infty}R[(a_j)_{j\leq i}]=\bigcup R_j, \quad R_j\subseteq R_{j+1}\subseteq K. \] By assumption, there exists finite \(j\) such that \(R_j=K\). Hence \(K\) is finitely generated over \(R\) and then is a G-domain.

Exercise 6

Let \(K\) be any field (not necessarily algebraically closed). Prove that any maximal ideal in \(K[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\) can be generated by \(n\) elements.

Proof. Use induction on \(n\); \(K[x_1]\) is PID so any maximal ideal can be generated by one element. Assume that the statement holds when \(n=k\); when \(n=k+1\), let \(\mathfrak{m}\) be a maximal ideal in \(K[x_1,\cdots,x_{k+1}]=K[x_1,\cdots,x_k][x_{k+1}]\). Then \(\mathfrak{m}\cap K[x_1,\cdots,x_k]\) is a G-ideal, hence a maximal ideal. By induction hypothesis, it follows that \(\mathfrak{m}\cap K[x_1,\cdots,x_k]\) can be generated by \(k\) elements. By Theorem 13, \(\mathfrak{m}\) can be generated by \((k+1)\) elements.

Exercise 7

Let \(R\) be a Hilbert ring such that every maximal ideal in \(R\) can be generated by \(k\) elements, \(k\) fixed. Prove that any maximal ideal in \(R[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\) can be generated by \(k+n\) elements.

Proof. Straightforward by induction.

Exercise 8

Let \(R\) be a Hilbert ring in which every maximal ideal is finitely generated. Prove that the same is true for \(R[x]\).

Proof. Straightforward by Theorem 13.

Exercise 9

Prove that the following statements are equivalent:

  1. \(R\) is a Hilbert ring;

  2. Every radical ideal in \(R\) is an intersection of maximal ideals;

  3. Every prime ideal in \(R\) is an intersection of maximal ideals;

  4. Every G-ideal in \(R\) is an intersection of maximal ideals.

Proof.

    1. implies (b): A radical ideal is equal to the intersection of G-ideals containing it; In Hilbert ring, G-ideal is maximal ideal;
    1. implies (c): A prime ideal is radical;
    1. implies (d): G-ideal is defined to be prime;
    1. implies (a): \(\mathfrak{p}=\bigcap \mathfrak{m}_i\); Since \(R/\mathfrak{p}\) is a G-domain, there is a non-zero element contained in each non-zero prime ideal in \(R/\mathfrak{p}\). Assume that for each \(i\), \(\mathfrak{p}\) is properly contained in \(\mathfrak{m}_i\). Thus \(\overline{\mathfrak{m}_i}\neq \{0\}\) is non-zero prime ideal. However \(\bigcap_i\overline{\mathfrak{m}_i}=\overline{\bigcap_i \mathfrak{m}_i}=\overline{\mathfrak{p}}=\{0\}\), a contradiction. Hence \(\mathfrak{p}\) is equal to one of \(\mathfrak{m}_i\)’s, hence a maxiaml ideal.
Exercise 10

Let \(R\) be any commutative ring. Let \(\mathfrak{m}\) be a maximal ideal in \(T=R[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\) satisfying \(\mathfrak{m}\cap R=0\). Prove that \(\mathfrak{m}\) can be generated by \(n+1\) elements.

Proof. Denote by \(v_i\) the image of \(x_i\) under the canonical homomorphism:\(T\twoheadrightarrow T/\mathfrak{m}\). As \(\mathfrak{m}\cap R=0\), \(T/\mathfrak{m}\cong R[v_1,\cdots,v_n]\). Therefore \(R\) is a G-domain. Let \(K\) be the quotient field of \(R\) and it follows from \(R\) is a G-domain that \(K=R[u^{-1}]\) for some \(u\in R\). As \(T/\mathfrak{m}\) is a field there exists \(g\in T\) such that \(ug\equiv 1 \, (\operatorname{mod} \mathfrak{m})\). Pass to the ring \(T/(ug-1)\cong R[u^{-1},x_1,\cdots,x_n]\cong K[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\). Therefore, by Exercise 6, \(\mathfrak{m} \operatorname{mod} (ug-1)\) can be generated by \(n\) more elements. Hence \(\mathfrak{m}\) can be generated by \(n+1\) elements.

Exercise 11

Let \(K\) be algebraically closed and \(R=K[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\). Let \(f_1,\dots,f_m\in R\). Then the equations \(f_1=0,f_2=0,\dots,f_m=0\) have a simultaneous solution if and only if there do not exist \(g_1,\dots,g_m \in R\) with \(\sum g_i f_i =1\).

Proof. If \(\sum g_i f_i =1\) then clearly \(f_i\) have no simultaneous solutions; Conversely, if \((f_1,\cdots, f_m)\neq (1)\), there exists a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak{m}\supseteq (f_1,\cdots,f_m)\). By Nullstellensatz, \(\mathfrak{m}\) admits a form \((x_1-a_1,\dots,x_n-a_n)\); i.e. for any \(f\in \mathfrak{m}\), it follows that \(f(a_1,\cdots,a_n)=0\). Hence \(f_1,\dots,f_m\) have a simultaneous solution.

Exercise 12

Let \(K\) be a field and write \(A\), for the vector space of \(n\)-ples over \(K\). Given an ideal \(I\subset K[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\) we write \(V(I)\) for the subset of \(A\) consisting of all points in \(A\), where all the polynomials in \(I\) vanish, and we call such a set a variety. Given a subset \(S\) of \(A\), we write \(J(S)\) for the ideal of polynomials vanishing on \(S\). Assume that \(K\) is algebraically closed.

  1. Prove that \(J(V(I))\) is the radical of \(I\).

  2. Prove that the map \(S\rightarrow J(S)\) sets up a one-to-one correspondence between all varieties and all radical ideals.

  3. Prove that under set-theoretic inclusion the set of radical ideals is a complete lattice and that the set of varieties likewise is a complete lattice. Prove further that the correspondence of part (2) is order-inverting.

  4. In the lattice of varieties of part (3), prove that the union of a finite number of varieties is just the set-theoretic union.

  5. Prove that in the correspondence of part (2) an ideal is prime if and only if the corresponding variety is irreducible, in the sence that it cannot be exhibited as the union of two properly smaller varieties.

Proof.

  • It follows from \(f\in \sqrt{I} \iff f^n \in I\) that \(\sqrt{I}\subseteq J(V(I))\). By Nullstellensatz, the reverse inclusion is true also.

  • Clear.

  • Set \(\mathcal{J}=\{\text{all radical ideals in }R\}\). For any subset \(\mathcal{S}\) of \(\mathcal{J}\), the ideal \(\bigcap I_s\) is expected to be the \(\bigwedge \mathcal{S}\). It’s clear that the intersection of radical ideals is still a radical ideal. For any \(J\in \mathcal{J},J\subseteq I_s\), it follows that \(J\subseteq \bigcap I_s\). Hence \(\bigcap I_s=\bigwedge \mathcal{S}\). Define \(\bigvee \mathcal{S}\) to be the radical ideal \(\bigwedge\{J\in \mathcal{J}: I_s\subseteq J,\forall I_s\in \mathcal{S}\}\). By part (1), it’s harmless to write a varity \(V(I)\) where \(I\) is a radical ideal. It’s clear that \(\bigwedge\{ V(I_s)\}=V(\bigvee \mathcal{S})\) and that \(\bigvee \{V(I_s)\}=V(\bigwedge \mathcal{S})\). Thus the set of varieties is a complete lattice too. The last statement is striaghtforward.

  • Suppose \(p\notin V(I_1),p\notin V(I_2)\), then there exist \(f_1\in I_1,f_1(p)\neq0\) and \(f_2\in I_2, f_2(p)\neq 0\). Thus \(f_1f_2\in I_1\cap I_2\) with \(f_1f_2(p)\neq 0\); i.e. \(p\notin V(I_1\cap I_2)\), and hence \(V(I_1\cap I_2)\subseteq V(I_1)\cup V(I_2)\). The reverse inclusion is clear.

  • Write \(X=V(I)\) where I is a radical ideal. Suppose that \(X\) is irreducible and that \(fg\in J(X)=I\). Let \(Y=V(fg)=V(f)\cup V(g)\). Clearly \(X\subseteq Y\) and it follows that \(X=(V(f)\cap X)\cup (V(g)\cap X)\). Since \(X\) is irreducible, either \(V(f)\supseteq X\) or \(V(g)\supseteq X\), impling that either \(f\) or \(g\) is in \(I\). Thus \(I\) is prime. The converse is clear.

Exercise 13: The homogeneous Nullstellensatz

\(K\) is a field, \(R=K[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\). Call an ideal \(I\) in \(R\) is homogeneous if along with a polynomial \(f\), \(I\) contains the homogeneous constituents of \(f\). Call a variety is a cone if \((a_1,\cdots,a_n) \in V\) implies that \((ta_1,\cdots,ta_n)\in V\) for all \(t\in K\).

  1. If \(I\) is homogeneous, prove that \(V(I)\) is a cone.

  2. If \(V\) is a cone, and \(K\) is infinite, prove that \(J(V)\) is homogeneous. Give an example to show that we need to assume that \(K\) is infinite.

  3. Thus or otherwise, prove that when \(K\) is algebraically closed, the radical of a homogeneous ideal is homogeneous. (This is actually true for any field.)

Proof.

  • Write \(f=\sum_{i=0}^{N}f_i\) where \(f_i\) is homogeneous polynomial of total degree \(i\). Since \(I\) is homogeneous and \(f\in I\), we have \(f_i\in I\) for each \(i\). By definition, for any \((a_1,\cdots,a_n)\in V(I)\), it follows that \(f_i(a_1,\cdots,a_n)=0\). As \(f_i\) is homogeneous, \(f_i(ta_1,\cdots,ta_n)=0\) for all \(t\in K\).

  • Example: Consider the finite field \(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\). Let \(R\) be \((\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})[X]\). The principal ideal \(I=(x^3+2x)\) is not homogeneous since it does not contain its homogeneous piece \(2x\) while it vanishes on \(\{0,1,2\}\) which is a cone. As \(x^3+2x=x(x+1)(2+x)\), \(J(\{0,1,2\})=(x^3+2x)\).

  • Suppose that \(f=\sum_{i=0}^{N} f_i\in J(V)\) and \(a\in V\). Denote \(f_i(a)\) by \(a_i\). It follows from \(f(ta)=0,\quad \forall t \in K\) that \[ a_Nt^N+a_{N-1}t^{N-1}+\cdots+a_1t+a_0=0,\qquad \forall t\in K. \] Hence, \[ a_NX^N+\cdots+a_0\in K[X] \] has infinitely many solutions since \(K\) is infinite, which entails all \(a_i\)’s being zero.

  • Let \(I\) be a nomogeneous ideal of \(K[x_1,\cdots,x_n]\) where \(K\) is algebraically closed. \(V(I)\) is a cone by part (1); By part (2), \(J(V(I))=\sqrt{I}\) is homogeneous.

Exercise 14

Prove: an element \(x\) in an ring \(R\) is nilpotent if and only if it is a zero-divisor on every \(R\)-module.

Proof. Let \(M\) be an R-module and \(m\in M\). If \(x\) is a nilpotent, then there exists \(n\) such that \(x^n m =0\) while \(x^{n-1}m\neq0\). Then \(x(x^{n-1}m)=0\) and \(x\) is a zero divisor. Conversely, suppose that \(x\) is a zero-divisor on each \(R/\mathfrak{p}\). Then \(x\in \bigcap\mathfrak{p}\) and \(x\) is a nilpotent.

Exercise 15

Let \(K\) be a field, \(L\) a field containing \(K\), which is finitely generated as a ring over \(K\). Prove that \(L\) is finite-dimensional over \(K\).

Proof. Write \(L=K[a_1,\cdots,a_n]\) where \(a_i\in L\). By Theorem 7, it follows that \(K[a_1,\cdots,a_{n-1}]\) is a G-domain and \(a_n\) is algebraic over it. Thus \(K[a_1,\cdots,a_n]\) is finitely generated as a module over \(K[a_1,\cdots,a_{n-1}]\). By induction on \(n\), we have \(L\) is finitely generated as a vector space over \(K\).

Exercise 16

Let \(R\subset T\) be rings with \(R\) a Hilbert ring and \(T\) finitely generated as a ring over \(R\). Prove that any maximal ideal in \(T\) contracts to a maximal ideal in \(R\).

Proof. We have \[ R\xrightarrow{\iota}R[x_1,\cdots,x_r]\xrightarrow{\pi}R[x_1,\cdots,x_r]/I\cong T \] By Theorem 12, Theorem 17, it immediately follows that any maximal ideal in \(T\) contracts to a maximal ideal in \(R\).

Exercise 17

Let \(R_1\subset R_2\subset R_3\) be rings with \(R_3\) finitely generated as a ring over \(R_1\). Let \(I_3\) be an ideal in \(R_3\) and \(I_1,\, I_2\) its contractions to \(R_1,\,R_2\). Prove: if \(I_1,\, I_3\) are maximal, so is \(I_2\).

Proof. Clearly we have \[ R_1/I_1 \hookrightarrow R_2/I_2 \hookrightarrow R_3/I_3. \] The Exercise above applies to \(R_1/I_1\hookrightarrow R_3/I_3\) since \(R_3\) is finitely generated as \(R_1\)-algebra. Then we have \(R_3/I_3\) is a finite field extension of \(R_1/I_1\), which entails \(R_2/I_2\) being algebraic over the field \(R_1/I_1\). By Lemma 13, \(R_2/I_2\) is a field and thus \(I_2\) is maximal.
