ImportantExample

Roughly speaking, different geometric objects (in our intuition) cannot be distinguished by their topological structures alone.

The complex plane \(\mathbb{C}\) is homeomorphic to the open unit disk \(\mathbb{D}:=\{z:|z| < 1\}\). But they are totally different if we care about holomorphic functions on them (by Liouville’s Theorem).

Thus, it is suggested that we should consider topological spaces together with specific functions on them. From my perspective,these functions can be seen as local coodinates of space which may take values in a field \(k\) and we expect that there are rules of calculus like classcial calculus in \(\mathbb{R}^{n}\). Precisely prescribling the behaviors of these functions to achieve this goal is difficult for me. I will, nevertheless, first introduce the definition given in the textbook.

ImportantDefinition

Let \(k\) be a fixed field, \(X\) be a topological space. The space \(X\) is called a space with functions if every open set \(U\) of \(X\) is equipped with a \(k\)-algebra, \(\mathcal{O}_X(U)\), of \(k\)-valued functions on \(U\), satisfying the following conditions:

  1. Let \(U\) be the union of a family of open sets \(U_i\). Then a \(k\)-valued function \(f\) belongs to \(\mathcal{O}_X(U)\) if and only if every restriction \(f|_{U_{i}} \in \mathcal{O}_X(U_{i})\)

  2. Let \(f \in \mathcal{O}_X(U)\) for some open set \(U\). Then \(D(f):=\{u \in U:f(u) \neq 0 \}\) is open and \(\frac{1}{f} \in \mathcal{O}_X(D(f))\).

We will call the functions in \(\mathcal{O}_X(U)\) regular function.

Condition (a) implies that a function is regular if and only if it is locally regular.1

NoteExample

Consider the complex plane \(\mathbb{C}\) with Euclidean Topology and assign the ring of all holomorphic functions \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}} (U)\) to every open set \(U\). This is a space with functions defined above since \(D(f) =\{u \in U:f(u) \neq 0\}\) is open and \((\frac{1}{f})^{\prime}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f^{2}}\) on \(D(f)\).

In the example above, we consider the zeroes of a holomorphic function \(f \not\equiv 0\) on a open set \(U\), denoted by \(\{z_{i}\}_{i \in I}\). Then \(\frac{1}{f}\) is holomorphic on \(U-\{z_{i}\}_{i \in I} = D(f)\). By definition condition (a), \(\forall f,g \in \mathcal{O}_{C}(U), \frac{f}{g} \in \mathcal{O}_{C}( D(g))\) which is a meromorphic function on \(U\) as we defined below:

Suppose that \(U \subseteq \mathbb{C}\) is open. A \(\mathbb{C}\)-valued function \(f\) is called meromorphic on \(u\) if \(\forall p \in U\), there exists an open nbhd \(V_{p} \subseteq U, g_p,h_p \in \mathcal{O}(V_{p})(h_p \not\equiv 0 \ \text{on} \ V_{p})\), such that:

\(f\restriction_{V_{p}}=\frac{g_{p}}{h_{p}}\)

We see that, in definition, the meromorphic function is always a quotient of two holomorphic functions locally and in the example \(\frac{f}{g}\) is meromorphic where we take \(f_{p}=f\) and \(g_{p}=g\) for every point \(p\) 3.

Conversely: Does every meromorphic function on \(U\) admit such a representation? i.e. suppose that \(\cup U_{\alpha}\) is an open cover of \(U\) and \(\frac{g_{\alpha}}{f_{\alpha}} \in \mathcal{O}_{U_{\alpha}}(D(f_{\alpha}) )\) where \(g_{\alpha},f_{\alpha} \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}({U_{\alpha}})\), and further suppose that \(\textcolor{green}{\frac{g_{\alpha}}{f_{\alpha}}=\frac{g_{\alpha^{\prime}}}{f_{\alpha^{\prime}}} \ \text{on} \ U_{\alpha} \cap U_{\alpha^{\prime}}}\) (as functions) for all \(\alpha\), is there a quotient function \(\frac{g}{f} \in \mathcal{O}_{U}(D(f))\) where \(f,g \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(U)\) such that \(\frac{g}{f} \restriction_{U_{\alpha}}=\frac{g_{\alpha}}{f_{\alpha}}\) for every \(\alpha\)?

Yes! But it really depends on \(\mathbb{C}\) and it fails when the topological spaces are higher dimension complex manifords. Hence we omit the technical proof (depend on Werierstrass factoring theorem of Mittag-Laffer theorem).Therefore, we have:

\(\textcolor{blue}{\mathcal{M}(U)=\{\frac{f}{g}:f,g \in \mathcal{O}(U), g \not\equiv 0 \}}\)

TipDefinition

Let \(X\) be a topological space. A presheaf \(\mathcal{F}\) of sets on \(X\) is a contravariant functor from \(Top(X)\) to \(Set\).

\(Top(X)\): Obj:open subsets of \(X\) Morphism:inclusion map \(V \subseteq U\)

Recall that a contravariant functor satisfies the following conditions:

  1. \(\mathcal{F}(\emptyset)=\{*\}\), where \(\{*\}\) is the terminal object in \(Set\).
  2. \(\mathcal{F}(Id_{U})=Id_{\mathcal{F}(U)}\)
  3. \(\mathcal{F}(i_{U \subseteq V}):=\mathcal{F}(V) \to \mathcal{F}(U)\)
  4. \(\mathcal{F}(i_{V \subseteq W} \circ i_{U \subseteq V})=\mathcal{F}(i_{U \subseteq V}) \circ \mathcal{F}(i_{V \subseteq W})\)

Further suppose the following conditions hold, then \(\mathcal{F}\) is a sheaf:

  1. local axiom: Suppose that \(U_{i}\) is an open cover of open set \(U\) in \(X\) and \(s,t \in \mathcal{F}(U)\). If \(s\restriction_{U_{i}}=t\restriction_{U_{i}}\) for every \(i\), then \(s=t\). (wlog. suppose that \(U_{i}\subseteq U\))

  2. gluing axiom: Suppose that \(U_{i}\) is an open cover of open set \(U\) in \(X\), and if we have sections \(s_{i} \in \mathcal{F}(U_{i})\) such that \(s_{i}=s_{j}\) on \(U_{i} \cap U_{j}\) for evry pair \((i,j)\), i.e.\(s_{i} \restriction_{U_{i} \cap U_{j}}=s_{j} \restriction_{U_{i} \cap U_{j}}\), then there is a section \(s \in \mathcal{F}(U)\) such that \(s \restriction_{U_{i}}=s_{i}\) for each \(U_{i}\).

It’s straightforward to see that \(\mathcal{F}(U):=\{f:U \to \mathbb{C}: f \ \text{is holomorphic}\}\) and \(\mathcal{F}(U):=\{f:U \to \mathbb{C}: f \ \text{is meromorphic}\}\) are sheaves on \(C\) with Euclidean topology.

Importantspaces with functions VS sheaf

Condition (a) \(\Leftrightarrow\) local axiom and gluing axiom is straightforward.

As functions which are pointwisely defined, the local and gluing axioms are naturally satisfied (Just define the global function pointwisely). (But when the functions don’t agree on some intersection, like \(logz\) and \(\sqrt{z}\) on \(\mathbb{C}\), we cannot define a global function on the whole topological space. And maybe we should create a new and more natural underlying space to glue local functions)

In fact, the concept of Sheaf generalize local regular functions to local algebraic data 4. Provided the conditions (c) and (d), we can restrict data to smaller spaces. Provided axioms (e) and (f) we can obtain global data from local.

However, for general sheaves, \(\mathcal{F}(U)\) doesn’t carry any algebraic structures (\(k\)-algebra in spaces with functions).

NoteEx 1.1.1

If \(k=\mathbb{R} \text{ or } \mathbb{C}\) and \(X\) is any topological space,then \(X\) has a natural structure of a space with functions. Just take \(\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\) as the \(k\)-algebra of all continuous functions: \(U \to k\). Show that any continuous mapping \(X_1 \to X_2\) is a morphism.

NoteSol

Suppose that \(X_1 \xrightarrow{f} X_2\) is continuous and \(g:U \to \mathbb{R}(resp.\mathbb{C})\) is continuous. We want to show that \(f^{*}(g) \equiv g\circ f\) is continuous in \(f^{-1}(U)\). Let \(V \subseteq \mathbb{R}(resp.\mathbb{C})\) be an open set, then clearly \((g \circ f)^{-1}(V)=f^{-1} \circ g^{-1} (V) \subseteq f^{-1}(U)\) is an open set.

NoteEx 1.1.2

Prove that the identity of a space with functions is a morphism and the composition of morphisms is a morphism.

NoteSol

Suppose that \(X_1 \xrightarrow{f_1} X_2 \xrightarrow{f_2} X_3\) and \(g \in \mathcal{O}_{X_3}(U)\) \[ \begin{aligned} g \circ f_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{X_1}(f_{2}^{-1}(U)) \\ g \circ f_2 \circ f_1 \in \mathcal{O}_{X_1}(f_{1}^{-1} \circ f_{2}^{-1}(U)) \end{aligned} \]

NoteEx 1.1.3

Let \(U\) be an open space of a space with functions \(X\). Then show that the inclusion \(i:U \to X\) is a morphsim and if \(Y\) is another space with functions and \(g:Y \to U\) is a mapping, then \(g\) is a morphsim if and only if \(i \circ g:Y \to X\) is.

NoteSol

Suppose that \(i \circ g\) is a morphism and \(h(v) \in \mathcal{O}_{X}(V)\), then \((i \circ g)^{*} (h) \equiv h \circ i \circ g\) is a regular function on \(g^{-1}\circ i^{-1}(V) \equiv g^{-1}(V \cap U)\). We want to show that \(\forall f \in \mathcal{O}_U(V)=\mathcal{O}_X(V)\) (since \(U\) is open) \(f \circ g\) is regular. It’s clear since \(g^{*}(f) \equiv f \circ i \circ g\) is a regular function on \(g^{-1}(V)\).

NoteEx 1.1.4

Let \(f:X \to Y\) be a mapping between spaces with functions. Given open covers \(X=\cup U_{\alpha}\) and \(Y=\cup V_{\alpha}\) such that \(f(U_{\alpha}) \subseteq V_{\alpha}\). Show that \(f\) is a morphism if and only if each \(f_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha} \to V_{\alpha}\) is.

NoteSol

Suppose that \(f\) is a morphism. We have \(f^{*}(g),(g \in \mathcal{O}_{Y}(V_{\alpha}) )\equiv g \circ f\) is regular on \(f^{-1}(V_{\alpha}) \supseteq U_{\alpha}\). By defintion condition (a), \(f_{\alpha}\) is regular on \(U_{\alpha}\). Suppose that \(f_{\alpha}\) is regular and \(V \subseteq Y\) is an open set and \(v \in \mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)\).We have \(V=\bigcup{V\cap V_{\alpha}}\) and \(f^{-1}(V)=\bigcup{f^{-1}(V)\cap f^{-1}(V_{\alpha})}\supseteq \bigcup{f^{-1}(V)\cap U_{\alpha}}\) Then \(f \circ g\) is regular on \({f^{-1}(V)\cap U_{\alpha}}\)(since \(f_{\alpha}\) is morphism) and hence, by definition 1, \(f\) is regular on \(f^{-1}(V)\).
