Again, we assume the base field \(k\) is an algebraically closed in this section.

We will first show the following result:

\[ \text{The subring } A \subseteq \mathcal{O}(\Spec A) \ \text{is all of } \mathcal{O}(\Spec A). \]

in other words, we have \[ A \cong \mathcal{O}(\Spec A) \] where \(A\) is a finitely-generate \(k\)-algebra which has no nilpotents.

ImportantProof

Let \(f(x)\) be a function in \(\mathcal{O}(\Spec A)\). We need to verify that \(f\) is in \(A\). By definition, we have an open cover \(\Spec A=\bigcup U_{\alpha}\) by open subsets such that \(f(u)=\tfrac{g_{\alpha}(u)}{h_{\alpha}(u)}\) when \(u\) in \(U_{\alpha}\) where \(g_{\alpha},h_{\alpha}\) are in \(A\) and \(h_{\alpha}(u)\) is never zero on \(U_{\alpha}\). We may assume that \(U_{\alpha}=D(k_{\alpha})\) for some \(k_{\alpha} \in A\). Doing the rename \[ f(u)=\frac{(k_{\alpha}g_{\alpha})(u)}{(k_{\alpha}h_{\alpha})(u)} \text{ on } D(k_{\alpha}\cdot h_{\alpha})=D(K_{\alpha}) \] We may assume that \(h_{\alpha}=k_{\alpha}\).

Next consider the function \(h_{\alpha}^{2}f\). This equals \(h_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}\) on \(D(h_{\alpha})\) as \[ h_{\alpha}^{2}f=h_{\alpha}^{2} \cdot \frac{g_{\alpha}}{h_{\alpha}}=h_{\alpha}g_{\alpha} \text{ on } U_{\alpha} \]

Moreover both of them vanish outside \(U_{\alpha}\), and thus \(h_{\alpha}^{2}f \equiv h_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}\) as functions on the whole \(\Spec A\) and

Since \(\Spec A=\bigcup{D(h_\alpha)}\), by the corollary in the last section, it follows that \[ 1_{A}=\sum_{\text{finite}}a_{\alpha}h_{\alpha}^{2} \] for some \(a_{\alpha} \in A\). Therefore we have (use the technique called partition of unity where the result above comes into play): \[ f=f \cdot 1_{A}=\sum_{\text{finite}}a_{\alpha}(fh_{\alpha}^{2}) \equiv \sum_{\text{finite}}a_{\alpha}h_{\alpha}g_{\alpha} \] which is a function in \(A\). We have thus proved that any global regular function in \(\mathcal{O}(\Spec A)\) is in \(A\).

It remains to show that \(\Spec A\) is affine, i.e.

ImportantTheorem

\[ \operatorname{Mor}(X,\Spec A) \xrightarrow{*} \operatorname{Hom}_{k}(A,\mathcal{O}(X)) \] is a bijection.

NoteProof

Let \(\delta_{x}:\mathcal{O}(X) \to k\) be evaluation of a function at a point \(x\). Let \(f:X \to \Spec A\) be a morphism. Let \(a\) be an element of A (treated as the \(k\)-algebra consisting of functions) and \(x\) be a point of \(X\). Then, by definiton of \(f^{*}\), \(a(f(x))=(f^*a)(x)\), or, rather, \(\delta_{f(x)}\) is the composition \[ A\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\mathcal{O}(X)\xrightarrow{\delta_{x}}k \] It follows that \(f \neq g \rightarrow f^{*} \neq g^{*}\) as \[ f^{*}=g^{*} \Rightarrow \delta_{x}\circ g^{*}= \delta_{x} \circ f^{*} \iff \delta_{f(x)}=\delta_{g(x)} \iff f=g \] The last equality holds because that \[ \delta_{f(x)}(a)=\delta_{A\xrightarrow{f(x)}k}(a)=f(x)(a) \] i.e the point \(f(x)\) is the same as the homomorphism \(\delta_{f(x)}\). Thus we have proved that \(*\) is injective.

Conversely let \(\phi:A \rightarrow \mathcal{O}(X)\) be a \(k\)-algebra homomorphism. Define \(f \equiv r(\phi)\) by the formula \(\delta_{f(x)}=\delta_{x}\circ \phi\). We shall show that \(f\) is a morphism.

NoteExercise

Proof:

As \(f^{-1}(D(h))=\{x\in X : \phi (h)(x) \neq0\}\) where \(h \in A\), \(f\) is continuous.

Assume that \(h\) admits a form \(a(x)/b(x), a,b \in A\) on \(\mathcal{O}_{\Spec A}(D(b))\) and we expect that \(h\circ f \in \mathcal{O}_{X}(f^{-1}(D(b)))\).

Indeed we have \[(h \circ f)(x)=h(f(x))=h(\delta_{x} \circ \phi)=\frac{a(\delta_{x} \circ \phi)}{b(\delta_{x} \circ \phi)}=\frac{\phi(a)(x)}{\phi(b)(x)} \in \mathcal{O}_{X}(f^{-1}(D(b))) \]

Clearly \(r \circ * = \operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Mor}(X,\Spec A)}\) as \[ (r \circ *) (f)(x)=r(f^{*})(x)=\delta_{x} \circ f^{*}=\delta_{f(x)}=f(x) \] where \(x \in X\)

\(* \circ r = \operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Mor}(A,\mathcal{O}(X))}\) as \[ ((* \circ r)(\phi))(a)=(r(\phi)^{*})(a)=a \circ (r(\phi))=\delta_{r(\phi)(-)}(a)=\delta_{-} \circ \phi \circ a=\phi \circ a \] where \(a \in A\).

We have now constructed all affine varieties up to isomorphism which allows us to establish the following categorical result.

ImportantClaim

Show that the category of affine varieties over an algebraically closed field \(k\) with morphisms is contravariantly equivalent to the category of finitely generated \(k\)-algebras with no nilpotents with \(k\)-algebra homomorphisms.

Recall the definition of equivalence of categories.

Given two categories \(\mathcal{C}\) and \(\mathcal{D}\), we say that they are equivalent if there exist funtors \(\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{D}\) and \(\mathcal{D}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{C}\) such that \(\mathcal{F}\circ \mathcal{G} \simeq \operatorname{id}_{D}\) and \(\mathcal{G}\circ \mathcal{F} \simeq \operatorname{id}_{C}\). It is important to stress that equivalence is not equality; it allows objects to be isomorphic, not identical.

The following lemma is always useful to check that if two categories are equivalent:

NoteLemma

Given two categories \(\mathcal{C}\) and \(\mathcal{D}\) and a functor \(\mathcal{C} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{F}} \mathcal{D}\), \(\mathcal{C}\) is equivalent to \(\mathcal{D}\) if the functor satisfies the following two conditions:

  1. Fully Faithful:

    For any two objects \(A, B \in \mathcal{C}\), the map
    \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(A),F(B)) \] is a bijection (a one-to-one correspondence). This guarantees that the structure of morphisms (arrows) is perfectly preserved.

  2. Essentially Surjective:

    For every object \(Y \in \mathcal{D}\), there exists an object \(X \in \mathcal{C}\) such that \(F(X)\) is isomorphic to \(Y\) in \(\mathcal{D}\), i.e.
    \[ F(X) \cong Y. \] This ensures that no structure is left out.

NoteProof of claim

Consider the functor \[ X \to \mathcal{O}(X),f \to f^{*} \] Then we see these two categories are equivalent by the lemma.

In the first section we know that an open subset of a space with function is naturally a space with functions and the inclusion map is a morphism. In general let \(X\) be a subset of a space with functions \(Y\). Then \(X\) has an induced structure of a space with functions.

NoteDefintion

Explicitly give \(X\) the subspace topology. A regular function \(f\) on an open set \(U\) of \(X\) is a function of the following form:

There is an open cover \(U=\bigcup{X\cap V_{\alpha}}\) where the \(V_{\alpha}\) are open subsets of \(Y\) such that \(f(y)=g_{\alpha}(y)\) on \(X\cap V_{\alpha}\) where \(g_{\alpha}\) is regular on \(V_{\alpha}\).

NoteExercise

Check that \(X\) is a space with functions and the induction \(X\hookrightarrow Y\) is a morphism.

TipProof

Suppose that \(V \subseteq Y\) is open and \(g \in \mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)\). We expect that \(i^*(g)\equiv g \circ i\) is regualr on \(i^{-1}(V)=V \cap X\). Consider the values \(i^*(g)\) takes on this set:

for any \(x \in V \cap X\), we have : \[ (i^*(g))(x)=(g \circ i)(x)=g(x) \]

thus \(i^*(g)\) is the restriction of \(g\) on the subset \(X \cap V\) i.e. \(f \restriction_{X \cap V}\).

Take the open cover \(U=X \cap V\) (where \(\alpha=1,V_{\alpha}=V\)) and \(i^*(g)\) coincides with a regualr function \(g\) on the intersection \(X\cap V\).

With this notation we have

ImportantTheorem
TipDefinition of locally closed subsapce

A subset \(L\) of a topological space \(X\) is said to be locally closed at a point \(x\in L\) if there exists a neighborhood \(V\) of \(x\) in \(X\) such that \(L\cap V\) is a closed subset of the subspace \(V\). One says that \(L\) is locally closed in \(X\) if it is locally closed at each of its points.

Remark:

Let \(F\) be a closed subset of \(X\) such that for every point \(x\) of \(X\), there is a neighborhood \(V\) of \(x\) in \(X\) such that \(V\cap F\) is closed in the subspace \(V\); it then follows from Prop. 3 of I, p. 18 that \(F\) is closed in \(X\). On the other hand, it follows immediately that in general there exist locally closed sets that are not closed in \(X\).

Proposition 1 Let \(L\) be a subset of a topological space \(X\). The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. \(L\) is locally closed;

  2. \(L\) is an open subset of the subspace \(\overline{L}\), the closure of \(L\) in \(X\);

  3. \(L\) is the intersection of an open set and a closed set of \(X\).

A locally closed subspace of a variety is a variety called a subvariety. A closed subspace of an affine variety is affine and in this case a regular function on the subspace lifts to a regular function on the ambient variety.

TipProof
  1. A closed subspace of an affine variety is affine.

Let \(Y=\Spec A\) where \(A\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-algera with no nilpotents. A closed subset \(X\) of \(\Spec A\) is of the form \(\operatorname{zeroes}(P)=\operatorname{zeroes}(P\cdot A)=\operatorname{zeroes}(\sqrt{P \cdot A}), P \subseteq A\). Then clearly set-theoretically \(X=\Spec (A/\sqrt{P \cdot A})\). One simply checks directly from the definitions that:

\(\Spec (A/I)\) where \(I\) is a radical ideal of \(A\) has the induced structure as a space with functions.

Therefore \(X\) is affine if it is closed in an affine and clearly any regular functions on the closed subset is of the form \(f+I\) which lifts to a regular functon \(f\) (maybe not unique) on the ambient variety.

  1. If \(X \subseteq Y\) is open and \(Y\) is an affine variety then \(X\) has a finite covering by open affines.

As \(X\) is open, \(X=\bigcup{D(a_{i})}\) where the \(a_i\) are in \(A\). Then the \(D(a_{i})\) are the affines \(\Spec (A_{(a_{i})})\). To see that there are only finitely many necessary \(D(a_{i})\) we use

Note 1: Exercise

Let \(X\) be a subspace of a space with functions \(Y\). For any space with functions \(Z\) a mapping \(Z \to X\) is a morphism if the composition \(Z \to X \to Y\) is a morphism.

TipProof

Suppose that \(f : Z \rightarrow X\), \(i: X \hookrightarrow Y\) and the composition \(h=i \circ f:Z \rightarrow Y\). We expect to show that:

If \(h\) is a morphism, then \(f:Z \rightarrow X\) is a morphism.

  1. \(f\) is continuous.

Let \(U \subseteq X\) be open. Then there exists an open subset of \(V\) such that \(U=X \cap V=i^{-1}(V)\). Then \[ f^{-1}(U)=f^{-1}(i^{-1}(V))=(i \circ f)^{-1}(V)=h^{-1}(V) \] which is an open subset of \(Z\) as \(h\) is continuous.

  1. \(f\) pulls-back regular functions to regular functions.

Let \(U \subseteq X\) be open and \(g \in \mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\). We want to show that \(f^{*}(g)=g \circ f\) is regualr on \(f^{-1}(U)\).

As \(g\) is regular on the open subset \(U\) of \(X\), by definition, there exists a family of open subsets \(V_{\alpha}\) of \(Y\) such that \(U= \bigcup_{\alpha}(X \cap V_{\alpha})\) and \(g=g_{\alpha}\) on each \(X \cap V_{\alpha}\) where \(g_{\alpha}\in \mathcal{O}_{Y}(V_{\alpha})\). Next we use \(f^{-1}\) to pull-back the open cover \(U\) to \(f^{-1}(U) \subseteq Z\), i.e. \(W_{\alpha}=f^{-1}(X \cap V_{\alpha})\) is an open cover of \(f^{-1}(U)\).

On each open set \(W_{\alpha}\) we consider \(g \circ f\):

When \(z \in W_{\alpha}\), we have \(f(z) \in X \cap V_{\alpha}\), thus: \[ (g \circ f)(z)=g(f(z))=g_{\alpha}(f(z))=g_{\alpha}(i(f(z)))=g_{\alpha}(h(z)) \]

then on each \(W_{\alpha}\) we have \[ (g \circ f)\restriction_{W_{\alpha}}=g_{\alpha} \circ h=h^{*}(g_{\alpha}) \]

As \(h\) is a morphism we have \(h^{*}(g)\) is regular on \(h^{-1}(V_{\alpha})=W_{\alpha}\). Gluing up all the local functions we know that \(h^{*}(g)=g \circ f\) is regular on \(f^{-1}(U)\).
