The existence of affine varieties

Algebraic Geometry
Preliminary
Algebraic Varieties by G.Kempf
Author

Eugene Lee

Published

August 3, 2026

The main work of this section is to explicitly construct all affine varieties.

ImportantTheorem

If \(A\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra with no nilpotents, then there is a canonically constructed affine variety \(\Spec A\) with a natural isomorphism \[ A \cong \mathcal{O}(\Spec A) \]

Recall that \(A\) has no nilpotents(reduced) if \(a^{n}=0\) for some \(n > 0\) implies that \(a=0\) in \(A\). Since the \(k\)-algebra of \(k\)-valued functions is clearly reduced, the hypothesis of being reduced and finitely-generated is necessary for \(A\) to be an affine variety. Therefore this theorem constructs all affine varieties up to isomorphism. (for \(X \cong Y \quad \text{iff} \quad \mathcal{O}(X) \cong \mathcal{O}(Y)\))

As a set we define \[ \Spec A:=Hom_{k}(A,k) \] Before giving strcuture on this set, we shall look at the \(k\)-valued functions on \(\Spec A\).

A \(k\)-valued function \(f\) which admits the following representation (which is straightforward to see that is well-defined) is clearlt a \(k\)-valued fucntion on \(\Spec A\): \[ \begin{aligned} \Spec A & \xrightarrow{f} k \\ x & \mapsto x(a) \quad \text{for some} \quad a\in A \end{aligned} \]

TipLemma

There is a natural homomorphism \[ \begin{aligned} \phi:A & \to \{k\text{-valued-functions on} \ \Spec A\}\\ a & \mapsto (x \mapsto x(a)) \end{aligned} \]

By abuse of notation, we denote \(\phi(a)(x)=x(a)\) by \(a(x)\).

Let \(I \subseteq A\) be a subset of \(A\). Define \(\text{zeroes}(I):=\{x\in \Spec A : \phi(i)(x)=0 \quad \text{for all } i \in I\}\)

TipLemma

The subsets \(\{\text{zeroes}(I)\}_{I \subseteq A}\) form the closed subsets of a topology of \(\Spec A\).

TipProof
  1. \(\Spec A=\text{zeroes}(\{0\})\) and \(\emptyset=\text{zeroes}(\{1\})\)
  2. \(\text{zeroes}(I_{1}) \cup \text{zeroes}(I_{2})=\text{zeroes}(I_{1} \cdot I_{2})\)
  3. \(\cap \text{zeroes}(I_{i})=\text{zeroes}(\cup I_{i})\)

So far we have defined \(\Spec A\) as a topological space (together with the homomorphism \(\phi :A \to \{f:\Spec A \to k\}\)) and we introduce the regular functions on each open set now.

ImportantDefinition

If \(U\) is an open subset of \(\Spec A\), a regular function on it is a \(k\)-valued function such that there is an open covering \(U=\cup U_{i}\) such that each \(f\restriction_{U_{i}}\) admits the form \(\frac{\phi(a_{i})(x)}{\phi(b_{i})(x)},a_{i},b_{i}\in A,x \in U_{i}\) where \(\phi(b_{i})\) never vanishes on \(U_{i}\).

Clearly \(\mathcal{O}(U)\) is a \(k\)-algebra and satisties the condition (a) of definition of spaces with functions. For condition (b) note that \(D(f)=\bigcup (U_{i}-\text{zeroes}(a_{i})\bigcap U_{i})\) is open and \(\tfrac{1}{f}=\tfrac{\phi(b_{i})}{\phi(a_{i})}\) is regular on \(D(f)\). Since \(\phi(a)=\tfrac{\phi(a)}{\phi(1)}\), the image of \(\phi\) is contained in \(\mathcal{O}(\Spec A)\), that is, \[ \phi: A \to \mathcal{O}(\Spec A) \subseteq \{f:\Spec A \to k\} \]

NoteExercise

Show that, for any ring A, the set of nilpotent elements \(\sqrt{0}=\{a \in A: a^{n}=0 \ \text{for some} \ n>0\}\) is an ideal. And check that \(A=\sqrt{0} \iff A=\{0\}\)

TipProof
  1. Suppose that \(a,b \in \sqrt{0}\), we have \((a+b)^{m}=0\) for some \(m>0\) and \((ra)^{n}=0\) for any \(r\in A\). Hence \(\sqrt{0}\) is an ideal of \(A\).
  2. If \(A=\sqrt{0}\) then \(1^{n}=0\) which implies that \(1=0\).Hence \(A=\{0\}\).

NoteExercise

If \(A\) is a finitely generated ring with no nilpotents, let a be an element of \(A\). Consider the homomorphism \(\phi: A \rightarrow A_{(a)}\), where \(A_{(a)}\) is a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra with no nilpotents (Why?). Show that \(\phi\) induces an isomorphism \(\operatorname{Spec}\left(A_{(a)}\right) \cong D(a) \subseteq \operatorname{Spec} A\) where \(D(a)\) is given the open subspace structure as a space with functions.

TipProof

\[ \begin{aligned} \phi: A & \rightarrow A_{(a)} \\ x & \rightarrow \frac{x}{1} \end{aligned} \] We have \(\frac{1}{a},\frac{g_{1}}{a},\dots,\frac{g_{n}}{a}\) generate \(A_{(a)}\) where \(g_1,\dots,g_n\) generate \(A\) since if \(x \in A_{(a)}\) then it admits the form \(\frac{r}{a^{k}}=(\frac{1}{a})^{k}(f(g_1,\dots,g_n))\) for some polynomial \(f \in k[X_1,\dots,X_n]\), i.e.  \[ \begin{aligned} A_{(a)} & \cong \frac{k[X_{1},\dots,X_{n},t]}{(I,at-1)} \\ \text{If we say} \ A & =k[g_1,\dots,g_n] \cong \frac{k[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]}{I} \end{aligned} \]

If \(A_{(a)}\) has a nilpotent \(\frac{x}{a^{k}}\), then \(x^{m} \cdot a^{N}=0\). Then \((x \cdot a^{N})^{m}=0\) which implies that \(x \cdot a^{N}=0\) and we have \(\frac{x}{a^{k}}=\frac{0}{1}\) by definition of \(A_{(a)}\).

  1. As sets we have \(D(a)=\{x \in \Spec A:a(x)\neq0\}=\{f\in Hom_{k}(A,k):f(a) \neq 0 \in k^{\times}\}\) and \(\Spec A_{(a)}=Hom_{k}(A_{(a)} \rightarrow k)\). Consider the map \(F\): \[ \begin{aligned} F: \operatorname{Spec} A_{(a)} &\rightarrow D(a) \\ y &\mapsto y \circ \phi \end{aligned} \]

\(F\) maps into \(D(a)\) since \(F(y) \in Hom_{k}(A，k)\) and \(F(y)(a)=y \circ \phi (a)=y({a}/{1}) \neq 0\). (\(y({a}/{1}) \neq 0\) for \(y(1/1)=y(a/1 \cdot 1/a)=y(a/1) \cdot y(1/a) =1\)). Define its inverse \(F^{-1}\): \[ \begin{aligned} F^{-1}: D(a) &\rightarrow \Spec A_{(a)} \\ f &\mapsto (\tfrac{x}{a^{k}} \mapsto \tfrac{f(x)}{f(a^{k})}) \end{aligned} \]

It’s straightforward to show that \(F^{-1}\) is well-defined and belongs to \(\Spec A_{(a)}\). Moreover we have \(F \circ F^{-1}=id_{D(a)},F^{-1} \circ F=id_{\Spec A_{(a)}}\) as maps between sets.

  1. We have closed subsets of \(D(a)\) are of the form \[ \text{zeroes}(I) \cap D(a)=\{x \in \Spec A: i(x)=0 \ \text{for all} \ i \in I \subseteq A, a(x) \neq 0\} \] which implies that \(I \cap (a)=\emptyset\). And closed subsets of \(\Spec A_{(a)}\) are like \[ \text{zeroes}(J)=\{x\in \Spec A_{(a)}:j(x)=0 \ \text{for all} \ j \in J \subseteq A_{(a)}\} \]

Then do some calculus as follows: \[ \begin{aligned} &F^{-1}(\{m:A \rightarrow k | a \notin \operatorname{ker}(m), \operatorname{ker}(m) \supseteq I\}) \\ &=\{h \in Hom_k(A_{(a)},k)|(h \circ \phi) (a) \neq 0 , \operatorname{ker}(h \circ \phi) \supseteq I \}\\ &=\{h \in Hom_k(A_{(a)},k)|h(\tfrac{a}{1}) \neq 0 , \operatorname{ker}(h \circ \phi) \supseteq I \} \\ &=\{h \in Hom_k(A_{(a)},k)| \operatorname{ker}(h) \supseteq \phi(I) \} \end{aligned} \]

Therefore \(F,F^{-1}\) are continuous, thus homeomorphisms.

  1. To show that \(h \circ F \in \mathcal{O}_{\Spec A_{(a)}}(F^{-1}(U))\) First notice that \(h \in \mathcal{O}_{D(a)}(U)\) has the form \(h(x)=\frac{a_i(x)}{b_i(x)},x \in U_i,a_i,b_i \in A\), where \(U=\bigcup U_{i}\) and the denominator \(b_i(x)\) vanishes nowhere on \(U_i\).

Take \(t \in F^{-1}(U_{i})\). Expect that: \[ (h \circ F)(t)=\frac{a_i^{\prime}(t)}{b_i^{\prime}(t)},a_i^{\prime},b_i^{\prime}\in A_{(a)} \ \text{where} \ b_i^{\prime} \ \text{vanishes nowhere on} \ F^{-1}(U_{i}) \] Indeed \[ \begin{aligned} (h \circ F)(t)=h(F(t))=\frac{a_i(F(t))}{b_i(F(t))},F(t)\in U_i,a_i,b_i \in A \\ \frac{a_i(F(t))}{b_i(F(t))}=\frac{a_i(t \circ \phi)}{b_i (t \circ \phi)}=\frac{(t \circ \phi)(a_i)}{ (t \circ \phi)(b_i)}=\frac{t(\phi(a_i))}{t(\phi(b_i))}=\frac{a_i^{\prime}(t)}{b_i^{\prime}(t)} \end{aligned} \]

Hence \(F\) is morphism. Totally same for \(F^{-1}\).

NoteExercise

Show that the collection \(D(a)_{a \in A}\) is a basis of the topological space \(\Spec A\) and \[ D(a_1) \cap D(a_2)=D(a_1 \cdot a_2) \]

TipProof

  1. Suppose that \(U\) is an open subset of \(\Spec A\), then \[ U=\Spec A- \text{zeroes}(I)=\{x \in \Spec A: i(x) \neq 0 \ \text{for some} \ i \in I\}=\bigcup D(i)_{i \in I} \]

  2. \[ D(a_1) \cap D(a_2)=\{x \in \Spec A:a_1(x)\neq 0, a_2(x)\neq 0\}=\{x \in \Spec A: a_1(x) \cdot a_2(x) \neq 0\}=D(a_1 \cdot a_2) \].
