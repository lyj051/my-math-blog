Let \(f\) be a non-constant polynomial in \(k[X_1,\dots,X_n]\) where \(k\) is an algebraically closed field as usual.

NoteLemma

After making a linear change of variables of the form \[ X_1 \to X_1+\lambda_1X_n,\dots,X_{n-1}\to X_{n-1}+\lambda_{n-1}X_{n} \text{ and } X_n \to \lambda_n X_n, \lambda_n \neq 0 \]

we may assume that \(f\) is monic in \(X_n\); i.e. f admits the form \[ X_n^d+\sum_{0 \leq i <d}f_iX_n^{i} \] where \(d>0\) and the \(f_i\)’s are polynomials in the \(n-1\) variables \(X_1,\dots,X_{n-1}\).

Proof. Suppose that the highest degree of the homogeneous component of \(f\) is \(d > 0\) and the corresponding piece is \(f_d\); Then \(f=f_d+g\) where the total degree of polynomial \(g\) is less than \(d\). Consider the new polynomial \(f^\prime =f(X_1+\lambda_1X_n,\dots,\lambda_nX_n)\), we have \[ f^\prime=f_d(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)X_n^d+g^\prime，\operatorname{deg}(X_n)<d \text{ in any monomial of } g^\prime \] If \(f_d\) is a non-zero function on \(\mathbb{A}^n\), suitable \(\lambda\)’s make the homogeneous polynomial \(f_d\) take the value \(1\).

Thus we wish to show that a non-zero polynomial \(g\) in variables \(X_1,\dots,X_n\) defines a non-zero function on \(\mathbb{A}^n\).

The proof is by induction:

  • if \(n=1\), \(g\) has finitely many zeores on \(k\) which is infinite; thus the result is clear.
  • if \(n>1\), write \(g=\sum_{0\leq i \leq d}g_iX_n^{i}\) where \(g_i\) are polynomials in variables \(X_1,\dots,X_{n-1}\) and \(g_d\neq0\). By induction, we may find \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1}\) such that \(g_d(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1})\neq 0\); thus the polynomial of variable \(X_n\), \(g(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1},X_n)\), is not zero; by the case \(n=1\), we may find \(\lambda_n\) such that \(g(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_n)\neq 0\).

This completes the proof.

ImportantLemma

If \(A\) is a non-zero finitely generated \(k\)-algebra then we have an injection \(B \subseteq A\) such that \(B\) is a polynomial ring and \(A\) is a \(B\)-module of finite type.

Proof. Let \(k[X_1,\dots,X_n]\to A\) be a surjectice homomorphism with kernel \(I\). If \(I=0\), take \(B=A\). Otherwise let \(i\) be a nonzero element of \(I\). By assumption \(i \neq 0\), otherwise \(A=0\); By the first lemma, we may assume that \(i\) is monic of degree \(>0\) in \(X-n\) after changing the coordinates. Let \(A^\prime=\operatorname{im}(k[X_1,\dots,X_{n-1}])\) in \(A\).

Claim: \(A\) is a \(A^\prime\)-module of finite type.

\[ k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/(i) \rightarrow A \] is a surjective homomorphism. \(k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/(i)\) is a free \(k[X_1,\dots,X_{n-1}]\)-module of finite type; where the basis is \(1,X_n,\dots,X_n^{d-1} (\operatorname{mod}i)\); this proves the claim as expected.

As \(A^\prime\) has fewer generators than \(A\), by induction on \(n\), we may find a polynomial ring \(B \subseteq A^\prime\) such that \(A^\prime\) is a \(B\)-module of finite type. Hence by the claim \(A\) is a \(B\)-module of finite type.

We will need to know a special case of this argument.

NoteLemma

Let \(i\) be a non-constant regular function on \(\mathbb{A}^n\). We may find a morphism \(\pi:\{i=0\} \to \mathbb{A}^{n-1}\) which is surjective such that, via \(\pi^*\), \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^n}(\{i=0\})\) is a \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^n}(\mathbb{A}^{n-1})\)-module of finite type.

Proof. Let \(V(i)=\{p \in \mathbb{A}^{n}:i(p)=0\}\), then by nullstellensatz, \[ \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^n}(V(i))=k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/\sqrt{(i)} \simeq \frac{k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/(i)}{\sqrt{(i)}/i}\equiv B/\sqrt{0} \]

By the second lemma, \(B/\sqrt{0}\) is a \(k[X_1,\dots,X_{n-1}]\equiv A\)-module of finite type and indeed we have \(k\hookrightarrow A\hookrightarrow B\); By theorem，there is a surjective map induced by composition: \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{k}(B/\sqrt{0},k) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_{k}(B,k)\twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{k}(A,k) \] where the first equality follows from \(\sqrt{0} \subseteq \operatorname{ker}(f)\) for any \(f \in \operatorname{Hom}_{k}(B,k)\) (nilpotents must be killed since \(k\) has no nilpotents);

Since \(V(i)=\operatorname{Spec }(B/\sqrt{0})\), we have found the surjective map.
