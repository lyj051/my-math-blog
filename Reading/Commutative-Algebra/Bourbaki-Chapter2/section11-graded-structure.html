GRADED COMMUTATIVE GROUPS

NoteDEFINITION

Graded Commutative Group of Type \(\Delta\)

Given a commutative group \(G\) written additively and a set \(\Delta\), a gradation of type \(\Delta\) on \(G\) is a family \((G_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in \Delta}\) of subgroups of \(G\), of which \(G\) is the direct sum, i.e \(G=\bigoplus_{\lambda \in \Delta}G_{\lambda}\). Then the set \(G\), with the structure defined by its group law and its gradation, is called a graded (commutative) group of type \(\Delta\).

Caution

There is an another way to understand where the type comes from. Let \(G\) be a commutative group; let \(G=\oplus_{i\in I}G_i\) be a decomposition of it. Suppose that there is another set \(J\) (may be equipped with some structures) such that there is an injection (set-theoretically) from \(I\) into \(J\). Then \((G_i)\) is said to be of type \(J\).

Some Notations:

  • \(\Delta\) is called the set of degrees of \(G\). An element \(x \in G\) is called homogeneous if it belongs to one of the \(G_{\lambda}\), homogeous of degree \(\lambda\) if \(x \in G_{\lambda}\).

  • The element \(0\) is therefore homogeneous of all degrees.

  • If \(x \neq 0\), it belongs to only one of the \(G_{\lambda}\)’s by definiton of direct sum; the index \(\lambda\) such that \(x \in G_{\lambda}\) is called the degree of \(x\) (or sometimes called weight) and is sometimes denoted by \(\operatorname{deg}(x)\).

  • Every \(0 \neq y \in G\) may be written uniquely as a finite sum \(\sum_{\lambda}y_{\lambda}\) of homogeneous elements with \(y_{\lambda}\in G_{\lambda}\); \(y_{\lambda}\) is called the component of degree \(\lambda\).

Examples of Graded Groups

  1. Given any commutative monoid with identity \(0\) and a commutative group \(G\), a gradation \((G_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in \Delta}\) is defined on \(G\) by taking \(G_0=G\) and \(G_{\lambda}=\{0\}, \lambda \neq 0\); this gradation is called trivial.

  2. Let \(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}\) be two sets and \(\rho\) a mapping of \(\Delta \to \Delta^{\prime}\). Let \(G_{\lambda}\) be a gradation of type \(\Delta\) on a commutative group \(G\); for \(\mu \in \Delta^{\prime}\), let \(A_{\mu}\) be the sum of the \(G_{\lambda}\) such that \(\mu(\lambda)=\mu\); Clearly \(A_{\mu}\) is a gradation of type \(\Delta^{\prime}\) (by associativity of direct sum) on \(G\), said to be derived from \((G_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in \Delta}\) by means of the mapping \(\rho\).

  3. (Product of gradations) If \(\Delta=\Delta_1 \times \Delta_2\) is a product of two sets, a gradation of type \(\Delta\) is called a bigradation of types \(\Delta_1,\Delta_2\).

    • Suppose that the bigradation of type \(\Delta=\Delta_1 \times \Delta_2\) is on some group \(G\). For all \(\lambda \in \Delta_1\), let \(A_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\mu \in \Delta_2}G_{(\lambda,\mu)}\) and for \(\mu \in \Delta_2\), let \(B_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\lambda \in \Delta_1}G_{(\lambda,\mu)}\). Clearly \((A_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in \Delta_1}\) is a gradation of type \(\Delta_1\) on \(G\) and \((B_{\mu})_{\mu \in \Delta_2}\) is a gradation of type \(\Delta_2\) on \(G\); these gradations are called the \(partial gradations\) derived from the bigradationn \((G_{(\lambda,\mu)})_{\Delta}\). Note that \(G_{(\lambda,\mu)}=A_{\lambda} \cap B_{\mu}\).

    • Conversely, if \((A_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in G_1}\) and \((B_{\mu})_{\mu \in \Delta_2}\) are two gradations on some group \(G\) such that \(G\) is the direct sum of \(G_{(\lambda,\mu)}=A_{\Lambda} \cap B_{\mu}\), then subgroups \(G_{(\lambda,\mu)}\) consist of a bigradation of types \(\Delta_1,\Delta_2\) on \(G\), of which \((A_{\lambda}),(B_{\mu})\) are the partial gradations.

  4. Let \(\Delta_{0}\) be a commutative monoid written additively, with identity element denoted by \(0\); let \(I\) be any set and \(\Delta_{0}^{(I)}=\Delta\) denote the submonoid of the product \(\Delta_0^{I}\) consisting of the familys \((\lambda_{\iota})_{\iota \in I}\) of finite support. Let \(\rho:\Delta \to \Delta_0\) be the surjective homomorphism of \(\Delta\) onto \(\Delta_0\) defined by \(\rho((\lambda_{\iota})) \mapsto \sum_{\iota \in I}{\lambda_{\iota}}\). Given any gradation of type \(\Delta\), a gradation of type \(\Delta_0\) is derived by means of \(\rho\); it is called the total gradation associated with the given multigradation of type \(\Delta\).

    • For instance, consider the underlying group of \(k[x,y]\). Let \(\Delta_0\) be \(\mathbb{N}_{\geq0}\) and \(\Delta=\mathbb{N}^2\); let \((G_{(a,b)})\) be \(\{cx^ay^b:c \in k\}\);Clearly it’s a gradation of type \(\mathbb{N}^2\). By definition, the total gradation of this bigradution is just \((G_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in \mathbb{N}}=(\{cx^ay^b:c \in k,a+b=\lambda\})\).

GRADED RINGS AND MODULES

Henceforth we shall assume that \(\Delta\) is always a commutative mononid with identity \(0\).

ImportantDEFINITION

Graded Ring of Type \(\Delta\)

Given a ring \(A\) and a gradation \((A_{\lambda})\) of type \(\Delta\) on the additive group \(A\), this gradation is said to be compatible with the ring structure on \(A\) if \[ A_{\lambda}A_{\mu} \subseteq A_{\lambda +\mu} \text{ for all } \lambda,\mu \in \Delta \] The ring \(A\) with this gradation is then called a graded ring of type \(\Delta\).

NotePROPOSITION

If every element of \(\Delta\) is cancellable and \((A_{\lambda})\) is a gradation of type \(\Delta\) compatible with the structure of a ring \(A\), \(A_0\) is a subring of \(A\) (and in particular \(1 \in A\)).

proof:

As \(A_0A_0 \subseteq A_0\) by definition, it suffices to show that \(1 \in A_0\). Let \(1=\sum_{\lambda \in \Delta}e_{\lambda}\) be the decomposition of \(1\) into its homogeneous components. If \(x \in A_{\mu}\), then \(x=x \cdot 1=\sum_{\lambda \in \Delta}e_{\lambda}x\). Since \(xe_0 \in A_{\mu}A_{0}\subseteq A_{\mu}\) (\(\lambda + \mu =\mu\) implies \(\lambda\) must be zero, this is why we require that \(\Delta\) be cancellable), by the uniqueness of decomposition, \(xe_i=0\) when \(i \neq 0\) and \(xe_0=x\) when \(i=0\). Since this relation holds ture for every homogeneous element of \(A\), it is true for all \(x \in A\); in particular, \(1=1 \cdot e_0=e_0\), and thus \(1 \in A_0\).

ImportantDEFINITION

Graded Module of Type \(\Delta\)

Let \(A\) be a graded ring of type \(\Delta\), \((A_{\lambda})\) its gradation and \(M\) a left \(A\)-module; a gradation \((M_{\lambda})\) of type \(\Delta\) on the additive group \(M\) is compatible with the \(A\)-module structure on \(M\) if \[ A_{\lambda}M_{\mu} \subseteq M_{\lambda + \mu} \text{ for all } \lambda,\mu \in \Delta \] The module \(M\) with this gradation is then called a left graded module of type \(\Delta\) over the graded ring \(A\).

When the elements of \(\Delta\) are cancellable, it follows from Prop before that the \(M_{\lambda}\) are \(A_0\)-modules.

Clearly if \(A\) is a graded ring of type \(\Delta\) then the left module \(A_s\) is a graded module of type \(\Delta\).

TipRemark

The gradations most often used are of type \(\mathbb{Z}^{l}\); when we speak of \(graded modules and rings\) without mentioning the type, it is understood that we mean gradations of type \(\mathbb{Z}^{l}\).

GRADED POLYNOMIAL RINGS

NoteDEFINITION

Standard Grading

We will introduce a grading on the polynomial ring \(S=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\) over a field \(k\). Set \(\operatorname{deg}(x_i)=1\) for each \(i\) and \(\operatorname{deg}({x_1^{c_1}\dots x_n^{c_n}})=\sum c_i\) for each monomial. For \(i \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\),we denote by \(S_i\) the \(k\)-vector space spanned by all monomials of degree \(i\). In particular, \(S_0=k\). A polynomial \(u\) is said to be homogeneous if \(u \in S_i\) for some \(i\), and in this case we say that \(u\) has degree \(i\) and write \(\operatorname{deg}(u)=i\). Clearly we have a direct sum decomposition \(S=\oplus_{i \in \mathbb{N}}S_i\) of \(S\) as a group. Moreover \(S_iS_j \subseteq S_{i+j}\) holds then \((S_i)_{i \in \mathbb{N}}\) is a gradation of type \(\Delta=\mathbb{N}\) on \(S\). We say that \(S\) with this gradation is standard graded. The ideal generated by all polynomials of positive degree \((x_1,\dots,x_n)\) and it is called the irrelevant maximal ideal.

  • for instance, let \(A=k[x,y]\). In this case, \(A_0=k\), \(A_1\) is the \(k\)-vector-sapce of all linear forms, \(A_2\) is the space of all quadrics, etc. The polynomial \(x^3y^2-2xy^4\) is homogeneous as its terms all of degree \(5\). The polynomial \(x^3y^2-2xy^4+2y^3-10x^4\) is not.

  • The Laurant polynomial ring

if \(A\) is a ring; let \(R=A[X,X^{-1}]\) be the ring of Laurant polynomials with the indeterminate \(X\). An element of \(R\) is of the form \(\sum_{i\geq m}a_iX^i\) with \(m \in \mathbb{Z}\) and finitely many non-zero \(a_i\)’s. Clearly \((AX^i)_{i \in \mathbb{Z}}\) is a gradation of type \(\mathbb{Z}\) on \(A\).

  • Generally consider the ring \(R=A[(X_{i},X_{j}^{-1})_{i,j\in I}]\) spanned by \(\prod_{i \in I}{X_i^{d_i}},d_i\in \mathbb{Z}\) where \(d_i=0\) for all but finite \(i\) as \(A\)-module. Set \(A_i\) be the subspace \(A\prod_{i \in I}{X_i^{d_i}}\) spanned by one monomial. Clearly \(A_iA_j \subseteq A_{i+j}\). Thus,by example 4 before we can obtain the total gradation of type \(\mathbb{Z}\) from the multigradation of type \(\mathbb{Z}^{I}\); explicitly, the gradation is the submodule generated by \(A^{\prime}_d=\prod_{i \in I}{X_i^{d_i}}\) where \(\sum d_i=d\).

An ideal \(I\) in \(S\) is said to be homogeneous or graded if the relation \(f \in I\) implies all homogeneous components of \(f\) are in \(I\). However, we shall not restrict ourselves to polynomial rings. We shall study homogeneous ideals in general graded rings. - In \(k[x,y]\), the ideal \(I=(x^2+y^2)\) is homogeneous and \(J=(x+y^2)\) is not.

NoteLemma

Let \(A\) be a graded ring of type \(\mathbb{N}\), \((A_i)\) the gradation and \(J\) a proper ideal of \(A\). The following statements are equivalent:

  1. \(J\) is homogeneous.

  2. If \(f \in J\), then every homogeneous piece of \(f\) is in \(J\).

  3. \(J=\oplus_{i \in \mathbb{N}}J_i\), where \(J_i=A_i \cap J\).

  4. If \(J^\prime\) is the ideal generated by all homogeneous elements in \(J\), then \(J=J^\prime\).

  5. \(J\) has a system of homogeneous generators, i.e. \(J=\sum_{\iota \in I}Aj_{\iota}\) where \(j_{\iota} \in A_{\iota}\).

In this case, the \(J_i\)’s are called the homogeneous component of the ideal \(J\).

Proof:

  • \((1)\iff(2)\) holds by definition.

  • clearly \(\oplus_{i \in \mathbb{N}}(A_i \cap J) \subseteq J\) holds; Suppose that \((2)\) holds, \(f \in J\) implies that \(f_i \in J\) where \(f_i \in A_i\) and thus \(f_i \in A_i \cap J\); \(f \in \oplus_{i \in \mathbb{N}}(A_i \cap J)\) as \(f=\sum f_i, f_i \in A_i \cap J\); therefore \(J \subseteq \oplus_{i \in \mathbb{N}}(A_i \cap J)\).

  • clearly \(J^\prime \subseteq J\); if \((3)\) holds, \(\forall j \in J\), \(j=\sum a_{\iota}j_{\iota}\) where \(j_{\iota} \in A_i \cap J\) is homogeneous, then \(j\in J^\prime\); thus \(J \subseteq J^\prime\).

  • Assume that \((4)\) holds, clearly \((5)\) holds.

  • Suppose that \((5)\) holds; if \(f \in J\), since \(J=\sum_{\iota \in I}Aj_{\iota}\) is a direct sum decomposition, \(f=\sum_{\iota \in I}a_{\iota}j_{\iota}\) where \(a_{\iota} \in A\) is zero for all but finitely many \(\iota\). Write \(a_{\iota}\) in the form of sum of homogeneous pieces: \(a_{\iota}=\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}}a_{\iota_{n}},a_{\iota_{n}}\in A_n\); Using the technique of swapping the order of summation, we have \[ f=\sum_{\iota \in I}a_{\iota}j_{\iota}=\sum_{\iota \in I}(\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}}a_{\iota_{n}})j_{\iota} =\sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}}(\sum_{\iota \in I}j_{\iota}a_{\iota_{n}}) \] As \(\sum_{\iota \in I}j_{\iota}a_{\iota_{n}}\) is a homogeneous element, if \(f_j\) is one homogeneous component, \(f_j\) admits a form of \(\sum_{n^\prime}\sum_{\iota \in I}j_{\iota}a_{\iota_{n^\prime}}\) where \(n^\prime\)’s are the indexs such that \(\sum_{\iota \in I}j_{\iota}a_{\iota_{n}} \in A_j\); Therefore \(f_j \in J\).
