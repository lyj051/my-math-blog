Definition 1: Products and Coproducts

Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be any category and \(X,Y\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\). Then the product of \(X\) and \(Y\), denoted by \(X\times Y\), is an object \(Z\in \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\) together with maps \(\pi_X:Z\times X\) and \(\pi_Y:Z\times Y\) such that any object \(T\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\), there is an isomorphism \(\operatorname{Hom}(T,Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}(T,X)\times \operatorname{Hom}(T,Y)\) given by \(f\mapsto (\pi_X\circ f,\pi_Y\circ f)\).

Similarly, the coproduct \(X\sqcup Y\) is defined such that \(\operatorname{Hom}(X\sqcup Y,T)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}(X,T)\times \operatorname{Hom}(Y,T)\) for any object \(T\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\).

Example 2

Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be the category of commutative \(k\)-algebras. The product of \(A,B\) is just \(A\times B\) where the operations are defined as: \[ (a,b)\times(c,d)=(ac,bd); \qquad (a,b)+(c,d)=(a+c,b+d). \] The coproduct is the tensor product \(A\otimes_k B\) where the bijection is defined as: \[ \begin{aligned} &f\mapsto (f\circ \iota_A,f\circ\iota_B); \\ &\iota_A(a)=a\otimes 1_B,\, \iota_B(b)=1_A\otimes b. \end{aligned} \]

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two spaces with functions. We will define a categorical product of them.

Lemma 1

There exists a space with functions \(X\times Y\) with two morphisms \(\pi_X:X\times Y\rightarrow X\) and \(\pi_Y:X\times Y\rightarrow Y\) such that for any space with functions \(Z\) the mapping: \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Mor}(Z,X\times Y)&\rightarrow \operatorname{Mor}(Z, X)\times \operatorname{Mor}(Z,Y)\\ f&\mapsto (\pi_X\circ f,\pi_Y\circ f) \end{aligned} \] is bijective.

Exercise 1

Prove in detail that this space with functions \(X\times Y\) satisfies the property of lemma.

Proposition 2

  • If \(U\) and \(V\) are subspaces with functions of \(X\) and \(Y\), then \(U\times V\) is a subsapce with functions of \(X\times Y\).

  • \(U\times V\) is open in \(X\times Y\) if \(U\) and \(V\) are open and similarly with closed.

Proof. As a set \(X\times Y\) is just the set-theoretic product of sets \(X\) and \(Y\); However, the topological structure on it is differenti from the product topology of the two topological spaces. In fact, we shall define more open sets in the space than the product topology.

We define a basis of open sets on \(X\times Y\) to be those subsets of the form \(U\subset V_1\times V_2\), where \(V_1\subset X,\, V_2\subset Y\) are open and \(U\) is the complement to the \(\operatorname{zeores}(f=\sum f_ig_i)\) where \(f_i\) are regular on \(V_1\) and \(g_i\) are regular on \(V_2\). Clearly we must have all of these open subsets if \(\pi_X\) and \(\pi_Y\) are morphisms and \(X\times Y\) is a space with functions.

Theorem 3

  1. If \(X\) and \(Y\) are varieties then \(X\times Y\) is a variety.

  2. If \(X\) and \(Y\) are affine, then \(X\times Y\) is affine and \(\mathcal{O}(X\times Y)=\mathcal{O}(X)\otimes_k \mathcal{O}(Y)\).

Lemma 4

If \(A\) and \(B\) are nilpotent-free \(k\)-algebras (\(k\) is an algebraically closed field), so is \(A\otimes_k B\).

Proof. We check that \(A\otimes_k B\) injects into \(\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Spec}A\times \operatorname{Spec}B)\) by homomorphism \(\sum a_i\otimes b_i \mapsto \sum a_ib_i\). For contradiction, take a nonzero element \(\sum a_i\otimes b_i\in A\otimes_k B\) in the kernel. W.L.O.G the \(a_i\) are linearly independent, as well as the \(b_i\). Find \(x\in\operatorname{Spec}A\) such that for some \(i\), \(a_i(x)\neq 0\). Restricting to \(\{x\}\times \operatorname{Spec} B\), we get a contradiction to linear independence of \(b_i\)’s. Therefore, we can identify \(A\otimes_k B\) with a supspace of \(\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{Spec}A \times \operatorname{Spec} B)\) which clearly contains no nilpotents.

Proof. If \(X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r} X_i\) and \(Y=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}Y_j\) are affine open covers then \(X\times Y=\bigcup_{i,j}X_i\times Y_j\) is a finite open covering. It (b) is true, each \(X_i\times Y_j\) is affine and (a) follows.

Suppose that \(X=\operatorname{Spec} A\) and \(Y=\operatorname{Spec} B\). By the lemma above, \(\operatorname{Spec} (A\otimes_k B)\) makes sense and it’s affine. By definition of affine variety, for any space \(X\), it follows that \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Mor}(X,\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_k B))&\cong \operatorname{Hom}_k(A\otimes_k B,\mathcal{O}(X))\\ &=\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,\mathcal{O}(X))\times \operatorname{Hom}_k(B,\mathcal{O}(X)) \\ &=\operatorname{Mor}(X,\operatorname{Spec}A)\times \operatorname{Mor}(X,\operatorname{Spec}B)\\ &=\operatorname{Mor}(X,\operatorname{Spec}A\times\operatorname{Spec}B) \end{aligned} \] Hence \(\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_k B)\cong \operatorname{Spec}A \times \operatorname{Spec} B\).

The moral is: one computes easily with tensor products of \(k\)-algebras to prove the relevant properties about products, but set-theoretic properties about products are easy geometrically.

Exercise 2

Prove that the products of quasi-affine varieties are quasi-affine.

Proof. W.L.O.G. suppose that \(U\subset X,V\subset Y\) are open subspaces of affines. Thus by proposition it follows that \(U\times V\subset X\times Y\) is also an open subspace of the product \(X\times Y\) which is affine.

Exercise 3

Show \(\mathbb{A}^n=\mathbb{A}^1\times\ldots\times \mathbb{A}^1\) (\(n\) times).

Proof. It follows that \[ \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^n)=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]=k[x_1]\otimes_k\ldots\otimes k[x_n]=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^1)\otimes_k\ldots\otimes_k\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^1). \] And the result holds for \(\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_k B)=\operatorname{Spec}A\times \operatorname{Spec}B\).

Theorem 5

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are varieties then \(\dim(X\times Y)=\dim X+\dim Y\) and \(X\times Y\) is irreducible if \(X\) and \(Y\) are.

Lemma 6: geometric interpretation of noether’s normalisation lemma

Let \(X\) be an affine variety with \(\dim X=n\). Then there exists a finite surjective morphism \(X\twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n\).

Proof. Let \(\mathcal{O}(X)=A\) which is a finitely generated \(k\)-algebra. By Noether’s normalisation lemma, there is a polynomial ring \(k[x_1,\dots,x_t]\hookrightarrow A\) such that \(A\) is a \(k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\)-algebra of finite type. It follows that there exists a finite and surjective morphism \(X\twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}(k[x_1,\dots,x_t])=\mathbb{A}^t\). By lemma, \(n=\dim X=\dim \mathbb{A}^t=t\).

Proof. Reduce to the case of two affines \(X,Y\). By lemma above, there are \(f:X\twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n\) and \(g:Y\twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{A}^m\). Hence, there is finite and surjective morphism \(X\times Y\twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n\times\mathbb{A}^m=\mathbb{A}^{n+m}\). (since \(\mathcal{O}(X)\otimes_k\mathcal{O}(Y)\hookleftarrow k[x_1,\dots,x_n]\otimes_k k[y_1,\dots,y_m]\) is a \(k[(x_i,y_j)]\)-module of finite type.) Consequently \(\dim (X\times Y)=n+m=\dim X+\dim Y\).

Assume that \(X\times Y\) is not irreducible. Then there exist closed subsets \(Z,W\subsetneq X\times Y\) such that \(Z\cup W=X\times Y\). Choose any element \(x\in X\) and then \(\{x\}\times Y\) is irreducible as \(Y\) is irreducible. Since \[ \{x\}\times Y=(Z\cap \{x\}\times Y)\cup (W\cap \{x\}\times Y) \] it follows that for each \(x\) either \(\{x\}\times Y\subseteq Z\) or \(\{x\}\times Y\subseteq W\). Set \[ X_1=\{x\in X:\{x\}\times Y\subseteq Z\}; X_2=\{x\in X:\{x\}\times Y\subseteq W\} \] write \[ X_1=\bigcap_{y\in Y}\{x:(x,y)\in Z\}; X_2=\bigcap_{y\in Y}\{x:(x,y)\in W\}. \] As for each \(y\in Y\), \(X\times\{y\}\) is closed, the intersection \(\{x:(x,y)\in Z\}=X\times\{y\}\cap Z\) is closed. Consequently, \(X_1,X_2\) are closed subsets in \(X\). And \(X_1\cup X_2=X\). Since \(X\) is irreducible, either \(X_1\) or \(X_2\) is equal to \(X\). Hence \(X\times Y\) is equal to \(Z\) or \(W\), which contradicts the assumption that \(Z,W\) are proper subset.
