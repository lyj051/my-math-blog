Technical Lemmas II

The lemmas here are mainly taken from I.Kaplansky-Commutative-Rings (1974.UCP).

Charaterizations of Prime ideals

One should be familiar with the basic definitions of primes. Let \(\mathfrak{p}\) be an ideal and it’s said to be a prime if:

  1. \(ab\in \mathfrak{p}\) implies that \(a\in \mathfrak{p}\) or \(b\in \mathfrak{p}\).

  2. \(R/\mathfrak{p}\) is a domain.

  3. \(_{R}\complement \mathfrak{p}\) is a multiplicatively closed set.

Clearly \(\mathfrak{m}\) is prime. The general observation below is due to Krull:

Lemma 1

Let \(S\) be a multiplicatively closed set in a ring \(R\) and \(I\) be an ideal in \(R\) maximal w.r.t the exclusion of \(S\). Then \(I\) is prime.

The proof by contradiction is standard.

Proof. Let \(I\) be such maximal ideal. Assume \(ab\in I\) but neither \(a\) nor \(b\) is in \(I\). Then the ideals \(I+(a)\) and \(I+(b)\) must intersect with \(S\). It follows that \((i_1+ar_1)(i_2+br_2)=i_1i_2+i_1br_2+i_2ar_1+abr_1r_2\in I\) with \((i_1+ar_1),(i_2+br_2)\in S\). This contradicts the fact that \(I\cap S=\emptyset\).

Corollary 2

Given any ideal \(J\) disjoint from a multiplicatively closed set \(S\), it’s contained in a maximal ideal w.r.t the exclusion of \(S\).

Proof. By Zorn’s lemma and lemma 1.

We impose a further property on the multiplicatively closed set \(S\): it’s said to be saturated multiplicatively closed if along with an element \(x\in S\) it contains all the divisors of \(x\). We are led to the following lemma, in essense a sharpening of lemma 1.

Remark. It would have been reasonable to exclude the possibility \(0\in S\).

Lemma 3

The following statements on a set \(S\) in a ring \(R\) are equivalent:

(a). \(S\) is a saturated multiplicatively closed set.

(b). the complement of \(S\) is a set-theoretic union of prime ideals in \(R\).

Proof.

  1. implies (1) is clear; To prove that (1) implies (2), take \(x\in _{R}\complement S\). Then the principle ideal \((x)\) is disjoint from \(S\) since \(S\) is saturated. Expand \((x)\) into a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak{p}_x\) w.r.t the exclusion of \(S\) by Corollary 2 and it follows that each element in \(_R\complement S\) is contained in some prime ideal by Lemma 1. Therefore \(_R\complement S=\bigcup_{x}\mathfrak{p}_x\).
Example 1

Let \(S\) be the set of all non-zero-divisors in \(R\). \(S\) is a saturated multiplicatively closed set. So the zero-divisors in \(R\) are a union of prime ideals. The prime ideals maximal among there might be called the maximal primes of zero-divisors.

Roughly speaking, lemma 1 allows us to construct a prime ideal from a multiplicatively closed set.

In the next two lemmas we exhibit two ways of constructing primes without using a multiplicatively closed set. Lemma 4 is due to Herstein; lemma 5 and its immediate corollary, lemma 7 are due to I.S.Cohen.

Lemma 4

Let \(R\) be a ring and \(A\) be an \(R\)-module. Let \(Z\) be an ideal in \(R\) that is maximal among all annihilators of non-zero elements of \(A\). Then \(Z\) is prime.

Proof. Suppose \(\operatorname{ann}(x)=Z\) is such a maximal ideal. Then we expect that \(ab\in Z\) implies \(a\in I\) or \(b\in I\). Assume that \(a\notin I\) then \(\operatorname{ann}(ax)\supset I\). Since \(I\) is maximal, \(\operatorname{ann}(ax)=I\). Thus \(b\in\operatorname{ann}(ax)=I\).

Lemma 5

Let \(Z\) be an ideal in \(R\). Suppose that \(Z\) is not finitely generated, and is maxiaml among all ideals in \(R\) that are not finitely generated. Then \(Z\) is prime.

Proof. Let \(Z\) be such a maximal ideal. We make a indirect proof by the use of quotient of ideals. Assume that \(ab\in Z\) with neither \(a\) nor \(b\) in \(Z\). Then the ideal \(Z+(a)\) is properly larger than \(Z\) and consequently is finitely generated. As generaters for it we may pick elements of the form \(i_1+x_1a,\dots,i_n+x_na\,(i_1,\dots,i_n\in Z)\). Now let \(J\) be the set of all \(y\) in \(R\) such that \(ya\in Z\);i.e. the quotient of \(Z\) and \((a)\): \(J=[Z:a]\). Then \(J\supset I\) and \(J\supset (b)\), hence is properly larger than \(J\), hence is finitely generated.

Claim 6

Let \(Z\trianglelefteq R\) be any ideal and \(a\in R\). If \(Z+(a)\) is finitely generated, say its generators \(i_1+x_1a,\dots,i_n++x_na\), then it follows that \[ Z=(i_1,\dots,i_n,Ja) \] where \(J=[Z:a]\).

Clearly we have \((i_1,\dots,i_n,Ja)\subset Z\); it remains to show the reverse inclusion. Take an arbitrary element \(z\in Z\). Then all the more so \(z\) lies in \(Z+(a)\) where we can use the generators to express \(z\) as: \[ z=u_1(i_1+x_1a)+\dots+u_n(i_n+x_na),\qquad u_1,\dots,u_n\in R \] Since \[ (u_1x_1+\dots+u_nx_n)a=z-(u_1i_1+\dots+u_ni_n)\in Z \] by definition, we have \((u_1x_1+\dots+u_nx_n)\in J\). Hence \(z\in (i_1,\dots,i_n,Ja)\), as required.

By the claim we obtained that \(Z=(i_1,\dots,i_n,Ja)\), which implies that \(Z\) is finitely generated, a contradiction.

Ordering structure on the set of prime ideals

Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be the set of all prime ideals of a commutative ring. It seems reasonable to think of the partial ordering on \(\mathcal{S}\) as its first, basic structure.

Question 1

Can an arbitrary partially ordered set be the partially ordered set of prime ideals in a ring?

There is a first negative answer by the following lemma:

Lemma 7

Let \(\mathfrak{p}_i\) be a chain of primes in a ring \(R\). Then both \(\cup \mathfrak{p}\) and \(\cap \mathfrak{p}\) are primes in \(R\).

Proof. It’s clear that they are ideals. Our task becomes easier when we pass to the complements \(S_i\), it being quite evident that \(\cap S_i\) and \(\cup S_i\) are multiplicatively closed. Then they are prime ideals.

The partially ordered set \(\mathclose{S}\) of prime ideals has an another (slightly unexpected) property: between any two elements we can find a pair of immediate neighbors.

Lemma 8

Let \(\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq \mathfrak{q}\) be distinct prime ideals in a ring \(R\). Then there exist distinct prime ideals \(\mathfrak{p}_1,\mathfrak{q}_1\) with \[ \mathfrak{p}\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_1\subsetneq \mathfrak{q}_1\subseteq \mathfrak{q} \qquad (\star) \] such that there is no prime ideal properly between \(\mathfrak{p}_1\) and \(\mathfrak{q}_1\).

Proof. Using Zorn’s lemma, we can insert a chain \(\{\mathfrak{p}_i\}\) of prime ideals between \(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\) such that for any \(i\), if \(\mathfrak{p}_i\subsetneq \mathfrak{b}\subseteq \mathfrak{p}_{i+1}\) (\(\mathfrak{b}\) is a prime ideal) then \(\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}\). Take any element \(x\) that is in \(Q\) but not in \(P\). Define \(Q_1\) to be the intersection of all \(\mathfrak{p}_i\)’s containing \(x\), \(P_1\) the union of all \(\mathfrak{p}_i\)’s not containing \(x\). By lemma , \(P_1\) and \(Q_1\) are prime. Then clearly \((\star)\) holds. None of the \(\mathfrak{p}_i\)’s can be properly between \(P_1\) and \(Q_1\), for if \(x\in \mathfrak{p}_i\) then \(\mathfrak{p}_i\supseteq Q_1\) and if \(x\notin \mathfrak{p}_i\) then \(\mathfrak{p}_i\subseteq P_1\). By the maximality of the chain \(\{\mathfrak{p}_i\}\), no prime ideal at all can lie properly between \(P_1\) and \(Q_1\).

Lemma 9

Let \(T\) be an algebra over the commutative ring \(R\). Suppose that \(u,v\in T\) are commutating integral elements. Then \(u+v\) and \(uv\) are integral.

Theorem 10

Let \(T\) be a commutative algebra over the commutative ring \(R\). Then the elements of \(T\) integral over \(R\) form a subring of \(T\).

Lemma 11

Let \(R\) be a commutative ring, \(u\) an invertible element of a ring containing \(R\). Then \(u^{-1}\) is integral over \(R\) if and only if \(u^{-1}\in R[u]\).

Lemma 12

Let \(R\) be an integral domain contained in a field \(L\). If \(L\) is integral over \(R\), then \(R\) is a field.

Proof. For \(u\neq 0\) in \(R\) we have that \(u^{-1}\in L\) is integral over \(R\). By the lemma above, \(u^{-1}\in R[u]=R\). Hence \(R\) is a field.

Lemma 13

If \(R\) is an integral domain algebraic over a field \(K\) then \(R\) is a field.

Lemma 14

Let \(T\) be a commutative algebra over the commutative ring \(R\). The following statements are equivalent:

  1. \(T\) is a finitely generated \(R\)-module.

  2. \(T\) is a finitely generated ring over \(R\) annd is integral over \(R\).
