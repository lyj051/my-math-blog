Definition of affine and finite morphism

A morphism between varieties \(f:X \rightarrow Y\) is affine if there is an open cover \(Y=\cup U_i\) by affines \(U_i\) such that the open subsets \(f^{-1}(U_i)\) of \(X\) are affine.

If we furthermore choose the cover such that each \(\mathcal{O}_{X}(f^{-1}(U_i))\) is a \(\mathcal{O}_{Y}(U_i)\)-module of finite type then the morphism \(f\) is said to be finite;more precisely, \[ f_i^*: \mathcal{O}_{Y}(U_i) \rightarrow \mathcal{O}_{X}(f^{-1}(U_i)) \] is a homomorphism of finite type.

If \(Y\) is a point \(P\), \(f:X \rightarrow Y\) is affine if and only if \(X\) is affine and \(f:X \rightarrow P\) is finite if and only if \(X\) is a finite set.

As for embedding we have

Lemma 1
  1. Let \(i:X\hookrightarrow Y\) be the inclusion of a closed subvariety. Then \(i\) is finite.
  2. Let \(f\) be a regular function on a variety \(X\). Then the inclusion \(j:D(f)\rightarrow X\) is affine.

At first, let’s review the structure of a closed subsapce in an affine variety. (the proof is here)

Reminder 1

Let \(X\) be an affine variety.

  • Denote the \(k\)-algebra of all the (global) regular functions on \(X\) by \(A\) and let \(Y \subset X\) be a closed subset; Denote the ideal of functions vanishing on \(Y\) by \(I\); Then the charaterization of \(Y\) is \[ \begin{aligned} Y \cong \operatorname{Spec}(A/I); \quad \mathcal{O}_{X}(Y) \cong A/I \end{aligned} \]

  • A subsapce \(Y\subseteq X\) is an affine variety if and only if it’s closed.

Proof.

  1. \(i\) is affine for \(i^{-1}(U)=U\cap X\) where \(U\subset Y\) is any open affine is a closed subspace of the affine variety \(U\). Denote the ideal of \(\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\) vanishing on \(U\cap X\) by \(\mathcal{I}(U\cap X)\); then by Reminder, we have
    \[ \mathcal{O}_{X}(U\cap X) \cong \mathcal{O}_{X}(U)/\mathcal{I}(U\cap X) \] thus \(\mathcal{O}_{X}(U\cap X)\) is a \(\mathcal{O}_X(U)\)-module of finite type where the generator is just \(1 \,(\operatorname{mod}\, \mathcal{I}(U\cap X))\).

  2. Restrict \(f\) to \(U\); we obtain that \(D(f)\cap U=D(f\restriction_{U})\) and thus we have \(D(f\restriction_{U})\cong \operatorname{Spec} ([\mathcal{O}_X(U)]_{f\restriction_U})\) which is affine.

Topological properties of finite morphisms

Topologically finite morphisms are special.

Lemma 2

Let \(f:X \rightarrow Y\) be a finite morphism. Let \(Z\) be a closed subset of \(X\). Then its image \(f(Z)\) is closed.

Proof. As the property is local on \(Y\) we may assume that \(X\) and \(Y\) are affine and thus \(\mathcal{O}(X)\) is a \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\)-module of finite type. As \(Z\) is closed, we have the surjection \(i^*:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow \mathcal{O}(Z)\). Let \(I\) be the kernel of the composition \(\psi\): \[ \mathcal{O}(Y) \xrightarrow{f^*}\mathcal{O}(X)\xrightarrow{i^*}\mathcal{O}(Z) \] Then we have an inclusion: \[ A=\mathcal{O}(Y)/I \hookrightarrow \mathcal{O}(Z)=B \] and \(B\) is an \(A\)-module of finite type.

By definition a point \(y\) of \(Y\) is contained in the image \(f(Z)\) if and only if \(\delta_{y}:\mathcal{O}(Y)\rightarrow k\) is the composition of \(\psi\) with \(\delta_z:\mathcal{O}(Z)\rightarrow k\) for some \(z\) in \(Z\).

Details 1

\[ \begin{aligned} &y=f(z) \iff \delta_y=\delta_{f(z)} \quad \text{notice that here } Y \cong \operatorname{Spec}(A) \text{ for some algebra } A \text{ as } Y \text{ is affine } \\ & \delta_{f(z)}= \delta_z \circ \psi \quad \text{ for: } \\ &\delta_{f(z)}(h)=(h\circ f)(z)=(h\circ f\circ i)(z) \\ &(\delta_z \circ \psi)(h)=(i^* \circ f^*)(h)(z)=(h\circ f \circ i)(z) \\ & \Rightarrow \delta_y=\delta_z \circ \psi \end{aligned} \]

thus \(f(Z)\subset \operatorname{zeores}(I)\) and it suffices to show the reverse inclusion now. In other words if \(y\) is in \(\operatorname{zeroes}(I)\) then \(\delta_y\) factor into the homomorphism \(\overline{\delta_y}:A \rightarrow k\) by the first isomorphism theroem. By Lemma there is a homomorphism \(\delta_z:B \rightarrow k\) where \(z\) is a point of \(Z\) which can be extended to \(\overline{\delta_y}\); thus \(f(y)=z\), and we are done.

We have another property of finite morphisms

Lemma 3

Let \(f:X \rightarrow Y\) be a finite morphism and let \(Z_1\subsetneq Z_2\) be two closed subsets of \(X\) where \(Z_2\) is irreducible. Then \(f(Z_1)\subsetneq f(Z_2)\).

Before we prove this let us see what it says in a special case.

Corollary 4

If \(f:X \rightarrow Y\) is a finite morphism and \(y\) is a point of \(Y\) then the fiber \(f^{-1}(y)\) is finite.

Tipproof of Corollary

As \(f^{-1}(y)\subseteq X\) is a subspace of a noetherian space \(X\), it’s also a noetherian space. Thus it will suffice to show that each irreducible component \(Z_i\) of \(f^{-1}(y)\) is finite. Let \(z\) be a point of \(Z_i\). Let \(Z_i^\prime=\{z\}\). Then \(f(Z_i)=f(Z_i^\prime)=\{y\}\). Hence \(Z_i=Z_i^\prime\).

Proof. It suffices to show that the affine case; then \(\mathcal{O}(X)\) is a \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\)-module of finite type. Furthermore, we may assume that \(Z_2=X\) is irreducible and \(Y=f(X)\). Thus \(\mathcal{O}(X)\) is an integral domain and is finitely generated as a module over the ring \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\).

We need to show that there is a non-constant regular function \(g\) on \(Y\) such that \(f^*(g)\) vanishes on \(Z_1\) because this will show that \(f(Z_1) \subset \operatorname{zeroes}(g) \subsetneq f(X)=Y\).

As \(Z_1\subsetneq X\) is closed, there is a non-constant regular function \(h\) on \(X\) which vanishes on \(Z_1\). Consider the \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\)-submodule of \(\mathcal{O}(X)\) generated by \(\{h^i:i\in \mathbb{N}\}\). This is finitely generated over \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\) because \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\) is noetherian (\(\operatorname{f.g.}\,k\text{-}\) algebra) and \(\mathcal{O}(X)\) is a \(\mathcal{O}(Y)\)-module of finite type. Then for \(i>>0\), \(h^i=\sum_{0\leq j<i}(f^*g_j)h^j\) where \(g_j\in \mathcal{O}(Y)\). We may assume that \(g_0\neq 0\). Thus \(f^*g_0\) vanishes on \(Z_1\). \(g_0\) is the non-constant regular function as expected.
