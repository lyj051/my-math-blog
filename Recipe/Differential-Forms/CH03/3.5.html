Differential form on differentiable manifolds

Differential Forms and Applications
P.Do Carmo
Author

Eugene Lee

Published

September 19, 2026

We now extend for differentiable manifolds the notion of a differential form of Chapter 1. Given a vector space \(V\), we will denote by \(\wedge^k(V)\) the set of alternate, \(k\)-linear maps \(w:V\times\dots\times V\rightarrow \mathbf{R}\), where \(V\times \dots \times V\) contains \(k\) factors.

Definition 1

Let \(M^n\) be a differentiable manifold. An exterior \(k\)-form \(w\) in \(M\) is the choice, for every \(p\in M\), of an element \(w(p)\) of the space \(\wedge^k(T_pM)^*\) of alternate \(k\)-linear forms of the tangent space \(T_pM\).

Given an exterior \(k\)-form \(w\) and a parametrization \(f_\alpha:U_\alpha\rightarrow M\), around \(p\in f_\alpha(U_\alpha)\), we define the representation of \(w\) in this parametrization as the exterior \(k\)-form \(w_\alpha\) in \(U_\alpha\subset \mathbf{R}^n\) given by \[ w_\alpha(v_1,\dots,v_k):=w\big((df)_\alpha(v_1),\dots,(df)_\alpha(v_k)\big),\qquad v_1,\dots,v_k \in \mathbf{R}^n\,. \] If we change coordinates to \(f_\beta:U_\beta\rightarrow M^n,p\in f_\beta(U_\beta)\), we obtain that \[ \begin{aligned} (f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)^*w_\beta(v_1,\dots,v_k)&=w_\beta\big(d(f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)(v_1),\dots,d(f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)(v_k)\big)\\ &=w\big(df_\beta\circ(d(f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)(v_1)),\dots,df_\beta\circ(d(f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)(v_k))\big)\\ &=w_\alpha(v_1,\dots,v_k)\\ \end{aligned} \] that is, \((f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)^*w_\beta = w_\alpha\).

Definition 2

A differential \(k\)-form in a differentiable manifold \(M^n\) is an exterior \(k\)-form \(w\) such that for some parametrization \(f_\alpha:U_\alpha\rightarrow M^n\), the representation \(w_\alpha\) is a differential \(k\)-form in the open set \(U_\alpha\subset \mathbf{R}^n\) (hence, in all).

From the above, it follows that a differential \(k\)-form in \(M^n\) is the choice, for each parametrization \((U_\alpha,f_\alpha)\) of \(M\), of a differential \(k\)-form \(w_\alpha\) in \(U_\alpha\) in such a way that for another parametrization \((U_\beta,f_\beta)\), we have \((f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)^*w_\beta = w_\alpha\) in \(U_\alpha\cap U_\beta\).

It is an important fact that all the operations defined for differential forms in \(\mathbf{R}^n\) can be extended to differential forms in differentiable manifolds. In particular, if \(w\) is a differential \(k\)-form in \(M^n\), we can define the exterior derivative \(dw\) as the differential \((k+1)\)-form in \(M^n\) whose representation in a parametrization \((U_\alpha,f_\alpha)\) is the exterior derivative of the representation of \(w\) in that parametrization, that is, \((dw)_\alpha=d(w_\alpha)\). Since \[ dw_\alpha=d((f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)^*w_\beta)=(f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha)^*dw_\beta \] \(dw\) is well defined. The same holds for the wedge product of differential forms.

Definition 3

A vector field \(X\) on a differentiable manifold \(M^n\) is a correspondence that assigns to each point \(p\in M^n\) a tangent vector \(X(p)\in T_pM\). The vecote field \(X\) is said to be differentiable if for every different iable function \(\varphi:M^n\rightarrow R\), \(X(\varphi)\) is again a differentiable function.

Let \(f_\alpha:U_\alpha\rightarrow M\) be a parametrization of \(M\) and \(X_i=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i},\, i=1,\dots,n\), the basis associatied to the parametrization. Then a vector field \(X\) can be written in \(f_\alpha(U_\alpha)\) as \[ X=\sum_{i=1}^n a_iX_i \]

Since a vector field \(X\) on \(M\) is an operation on the space \(D\) of differentiable functions on \(M\), we can take the iterates of this operation. For instance, if \(X\) and \(Y\) are vector fields on \(M\), \(\varphi:M\rightarrow \mathbf{R}\) is a differentiable function, we can consider the functions \(Y(X(\varphi))\) and \(X(Y(\varphi))\). In general, such iterated opreations do not lead to a vector field, since they involve second derivatives of \(\varphi\). For instance:

Example 4

Let \(M\) be the real line with the standard differentiable structure. Let \(X=\frac{d}{dx}\) and \(Y=x\frac{d}{dx}\). Then \(X\) and \(Y\) are vector fields on \(M\). However, \(X\circ Y\) and \(Y\circ X\) are not vector fields, since they involve second derivatives of \(\varphi\). In fact, we have \(X(Y(x^3))=9x^2\). Assume that there is a vector field \(f(x)\frac{d}{dx}\) such that \(f(x)\frac{d}{dx}(\varphi)=X(Y(\varphi))\). Then we would have \(f(x)\frac{d}{dx}(x^3)=9x^2\), which implies \(f(x)=3\). Clearly, \(3\frac{d}{dx}\) is not equal to \(X\circ Y\). Similar with \(Y\circ X\).

However, the difference \(X\circ Y-Y\circ X\) is a vector field. In fact, we have

Lemma 1

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be vector fields on a differentiable manifold \(M\). Then there exists a unique vector field \(Z\) on \(M\) such that for every differentiable function \(\varphi:M\rightarrow \mathbf{R}\), we have \(Z(\varphi)=X(Y(\varphi))-Y(X(\varphi))\).

Proof. We first prove that if such a \(Z\) exists, then it is unique. Let \(Z_1\) and \(Z_2\) be vector fields such that for every differentiable function \(\varphi:M\rightarrow \mathbf{R}\), we have \(Z_1(\varphi)=X(Y(\varphi))-Y(X(\varphi))=Z_2(\varphi)\). Then for every differentiable function \(\varphi\), we have \((Z_1-Z_2)(\varphi)=0\). Since \(Z_1-Z_2\) is a vector field, it follows that \(Z_1-Z_2=0\), hence \(Z_1=Z_2\).

To prove the existence of \(Z\), we take a parametrization \(f_\alpha:U_\alpha\rightarrow M\) and write \(X=\sum_{i=1}^n a_iX_i\) and \(Y=\sum_{i=1}^n b_iX_i\), where \(X_i=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\) is the basis associated to the parametrization. Then we have \[ Z=\sum_{i=1}^n (a_iY_i-b_iX_i) \] where \(Y_i\) and \(X_i\) are the components of \(Y\) and \(X\) respectively in the basis \(\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}\).

Write it in local coordinates, we have \[ \begin{aligned} &XY\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^n b_j\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_j}\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^n a_i b_j\frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial x_i \partial x_j}+\sum_{i,j=1}^n a_i\frac{\partial b_j}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_j} \\ &YX\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^n a_j\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_j}\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^n b_i a_j\frac{\partial^2 \varphi}{\partial x_i \partial x_j}+\sum_{i,j=1}^n b_i\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_j} \end{aligned} \] hence \[ \begin{aligned} (XY-YX)\varphi&=\sum_{i,j=1}^n \left(a_i\frac{\partial b_j}{\partial x_i}-b_i\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i}\right)\frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x_j}\\ &=\sum_{j=1}^n\left(\sum_{i=1}^n(a_i\frac{\partial b_j}{\partial x_i}-b_i\frac{\partial a_j}{\partial x_i})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\right)\varphi\,. \end{aligned} \]

Definition 5

The vector field determined by the above lemma is called the bracket of the vector fields \(X\) and \(Y\) and is denoted by \([X,Y]\); clearly, it’s differentiable.

Proposition 2

Let \(X,Y\) and \(Z\) be differentiable vector fields on a differentiable manifold \(M\), \(a\) and \(b\) be real numbers, and \(\varphi\) and \(\theta\) be differentiable functions on \(M\). Then we have

  1. \([X,Y]=-[Y,X]\)

  2. \([aX+bY,Z]=a[X,Z]+b[Y,Z]\)

  3. \([[X,Y],Z]+[[Y,Z],X]+[[Z,X],Y]=0 \qquad (\text{Jacobi's identity})\)

  4. \([\theta X,\varphi Y]=\theta\varphi[X,Y]+\theta X(\varphi)Y-\varphi Y(\theta)X\)

The proof of these basic properties are straightforward.

There exists an interesting relation between exterior differentiation of differential forms and the bracket operation. For the case of \(1\)-forms, this relation is as follows.

Proposition 3

Let \(w\) be a differentiable \(1\)-form on a differentiable manifold \(M\) and \(X\) and \(Y\) be differentiable vector fields on \(M\). Then we have \[ dw(X,Y)=X(w(Y))-Y(w(X))-w([X,Y]).\qquad({\star}) \]

Proof. Set \(f:U\rightarrow M\) be a parametrization of \(M\) and let \[ X=\sum_i a_i\, \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i}, \quad Y=\sum_j b_j\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} \] be the expressions of \(X\) and \(Y\) in this parametrization. We first observe that \((\star)\) holds for \(X_i\) and \(Y_j\), then it also holds for \(\sum X_i\) and \(\sum Y_j\). Next we claim that if \((\star)\) holds for \(X\) and \(Y\), it also holds for \(\theta X\) and \(\varphi Y\), when \(\theta\) and \(\varphi\) are differentiable functions on \(M\).

To see that, we first notice that, by hypothesis, \[ dw(\theta X,\varphi Y)=\theta\varphi dw(X,Y)=\theta\varphi\left(X(w(Y))-Y(w(X))-w([X,Y])\right)\,. \] It follows that \[ \begin{aligned} &\theta X \left(w(\varphi Y)\right)-\varphi Y\left(w(\theta X)\right)-w\left([\theta X,\varphi Y]\right)\\ =&\theta X(\varphi w(Y))-\varphi Y(\theta w(X))-w\left(\theta\varphi[X,Y]+\theta X(\varphi)Y-\varphi Y(\theta)X\right)\\ =&\theta\varphi X(w(Y))+\theta X(\varphi)w(Y)-\varphi\theta Y(w(X))-\varphi Y(\theta)w(X)-\theta\varphi w([X,Y])-\theta X(\varphi)w(Y)+\varphi Y(\theta)w(X)\\ =&\theta\varphi\left(X(w(Y))-Y(w(X))-w([X,Y])\right)\\ =&dw(\theta X,\varphi Y)\,. \end{aligned} \]

Now it suffices to show that \[ dw(X_i,X_j)=X_i(w(X_j))-X_j(w(X_i))-w([X_i,X_j])=X_i(w(X_j))-X_j(w(X_i))\,. \qquad (\star^\prime) \] To show this, we write \(w=\sum_k w_k dx_k\), where \(dx_k\) is the dual basis of \(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_k}\). Then we have \[ \begin{aligned} dw(X_i,X_j)&=d\left(\sum_k w_k dx_k\right)(X_i,X_j)=\sum_k dw_k\wedge dx_k(X_i,X_j)\\ &=\sum_k\left(dw_k(X_i)dx_k(X_j)-dw_k(X_j)dx_k(X_i)\right)=\sum_k\left(\frac{\partial w_k}{\partial x_i}\delta_{kj}-\frac{\partial w_k}{\partial x_j}\delta_{ki}\right)\\ &=\frac{\partial w_j}{\partial x_i}-\frac{\partial w_i}{\partial x_j}=X_i(w(X_j))-X_j(w(X_i))\,. \end{aligned} \] and this proves \((\star^\prime)\), hence \((\star)\).

Remark. With essentially the same proof, we can show that if \(w\) is a differentiable \(k\)-form on a differentiable manifold \(M\) and \(X_1,\dots,X_{k+1}\) are differentiable vector fields on \(M\), then we have \[ dw(X_1,\dots,X_{k+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}X_i(w(X_1,\dots,\hat{X}_i,\dots,X_{k+1}))-\sum_{i<j}w([X_i,X_j],X_1,\dots,\hat{X}_i,\dots,\hat{X}_j,\dots,X_{k+1})\,. \] where \(\hat{X_i}\) denotes the omission of \(X_i\).

Definition 6

A differentiable manifold \(M\) is said to be orientable if \(M\) has a differentiable atlas \(\{(U_\alpha,f_\alpha)\}\) such that for every pair of parametrizations \((U_\alpha,f_\alpha)\) and \((U_\beta,f_\beta)\), the Jacobian of the change of coordinates \(f_\beta^{-1}\circ f_\alpha\) is positive in \(U_\alpha\cap U_\beta\). Such an atlas is called an orientation of \(M\).
