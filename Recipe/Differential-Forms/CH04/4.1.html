Integration on Manifolds

Author

Do Carmo

Published

October 10, 2026

\(\newcommand{\R}{\mathbf{R}}\) \(\newcommand{\w}{\omega}\) \(\newcommand{\d}{\operatorname{d}\!}\) \(\newcommand{\a}{\mathbf{a}}\) \(\newcommand{\x}{\mathbf{x}}\) \(\newcommand{\y}{\mathbf{y}}\)

In this section we will define the integral of a differential \(n\)-form on an \(n\)-dimensional differentiable manifold. We will start with the case of \(\R^n\).

Let \(\w\) be a differential form defined in an open set \(U\subset M^n\). The support \(K\) of \(\w\) is the closure of the set \[ A=\{p\in M^n:\w(p)\neq 0\}. \]

Example 1

Let \(\w\) be a \(n\)-form and \(M^n=\R^n\). Then \[ \w=a(x_1,\cdots,x_n)\d x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \d x_n=a(\x)\d x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \d x_n. \] Assume that the support \(K\) of \(\w\) is compact and contained in \(U\). We define \[ \int _{U}\w =\int _{\R^n} a(\x) \d\x \] where the right hand is the usual multiple integral of a function with compact support in \(\R^n\) as defined in here.

We will now proceed to the definition of the integral of an \(n\)-form on \(M^n\). To avoid convergence problems, it is convenient to assume that \(M\) is compact; then the support \(K\) of \(\w\), being a closed set in a compact space, is also compact. As we will see in a while, it is also necessary to assume that \(M\) is orientable, i.e., that \(M\) is covered by a family of coordinate neighborhoods \(\{V_\alpha\}\) such that the coordinate changes have positive Jacobians.

Let us assume initially that \(K\) is contained in some coordinate neighborhood \(V_{\alpha}=f_\alpha(U_\alpha)\). Then, if the local representation \(\w_{\alpha}\) of \(\w\) in \(U_\alpha\) is \[ \w=a_{\alpha}(x_1,\dots,x_n)\d x_1\wedge \cdots \wedge \d x_n, \] we define \[ \int _{M} \w :\,= \int _{V_\alpha} \w_{\alpha} =\int _{U_{\alpha}} a_\alpha \d\x, \] where the right hand side is an integral in \(\R^n\).

It may happen that \(K\) is contained in another coordinate neighborhood \(V_\beta=f_\beta(U_\beta)\) of the same family, and must show that the above definition is independent of the choice of the coordinate neighborhood.

For that, we can assume, by contracting \(U_\alpha\) and \(U_\beta\) if necessary, that \(V_\alpha=V_\beta\). Let the change of coordinates \[ f=f_\alpha^{-1}\circ f_\beta: U_\beta \rightarrow U_\alpha \] be given by \[ \begin{aligned} & x_i=f_i(y_1,\cdots,y_n),\quad i=1,\dots,n, \\ & (x_1,\dots,x_n)\in U_\alpha, \quad (y_1,\dots,y_n)\in U_\beta \end{aligned} \] Since \(\w_{\beta}=f^*(\w_\alpha)\), we obtain that \[ \w_\beta=\det(\d f)a_\beta\, \d y_1\wedge\cdots \wedge \d y_n \] where \[ a_\beta=a_\alpha(f_1(y_1,\dots,y_n),\dots,f_n(y_1,\dots,y_n))\,. \] By definition and change of variables, we have \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{V_\beta}\w_\beta & =\int_{U_\beta}\det(\d f)a_\alpha(f(\y)) \d \y \\ & =\int_{U_\beta}|\det(\d f)|a_\alpha (f(\y))\d \y \\ & = \int_{U_\alpha}a_\alpha \d\x =\int _{V_\alpha}\w_\alpha. \end{aligned} \]

Notice that without the hypothesis of orientability for \(M\), the sign of the integral of \(\w\) is not well defined. The choice of an orientation for \(M\) fixes a sign for the integral of \(\w\) which changes with the change of orientation.

Let us now consider the case in which the support \(K\) of \(\w\) is contained in no coordinate neighborhood. For that, we need some preliminaries, and before going into details, we will present a sketch of what we intend to do.

Given a covering \(\{V_\alpha\}\) of a compact differentiable manifold \(M\), we will construct a finite family of differentiable functions \(\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_m\) such that:

  1. \(\sum _{i=1}^{m}\varphi_i=1\),

  2. \(0\leq \varphi_i\leq 1\), and the support of \(\varphi_i\) is contained in some \(V_{\alpha_i}=V_i\).

The family \(\{\varphi_i\}\) is called a differentiable partition of unity subordinate to the covering \(\{V_\alpha\}\). (When \(M\) is orientable, we choose \(\{V_\alpha\}\) compatible with the orientation.)

Let us assume, for the time being, the existence of such a family. Assume furthermore that \(M\) is orientable. We will define the integral of an \(n\)-form \(\w\) on \(M^n\) as follows.

The support of the form \(\varphi_i\w\) is contained in \(V_i\). By a previous definition, it makes sense to write \[ \int_{M} \w =\sum_{i=1}^{m} \int _M\varphi_i \w \,, \] the only question being whether this definition is independent of the choices made.

Consider another covering \(\{W_\beta\}\) of \(M\) which determines on \(M\) the same orientation as \(\{V_\alpha\}\), and let \(\{\psi_j\},j=1,\dots,s\) be a partition of unity subordinate to \(\{W_\beta\}\). Then \(\{V_\alpha\cap W_\beta\}\) will be a covering for \(M\) and the family \(\varphi_i\psi_j\) will be a partition of unity subordinate to \(\{V_\alpha \cap W_\beta\}\). Thus \[ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\int _M \varphi_i \w =\sum_{i=1}^{m} \int _M \varphi_i \Big( \sum_{j=1} ^{s} \psi_j \Big)\w =\sum_{ij} \int_M \varphi_i \psi_j \w\,, \] where in the last equality it was used that, for each \(i\), the functions \(\varphi_i \psi_j\) are defined in \(V_i\). Similarly, \[ \sum_{j=1}^s \int _M \psi_j \w=\sum _{j=1}^s \int_M \Big(\sum_{i=1}^m \varphi_i\Big)\psi_j \w=\sum_{ij}\int_M \varphi_i\psi_j\w \] which proves the required independence.

Remark. What we have done was essentially the following. We observed that the integral of a differential form whose domain is contained in a coordinate neighborhood reduces to a multiple integral. To integrate differential forms in more complicated domains we can proceed in either of the two following ways; We divide the complicated domain in simpler domains, and add up the results, or we decompose the form into forms that are zero outside simple domains and add up the results. Since it is easier to work with functions than with domains, the second alternative is usually preferred, and it was the one used here.
